# 02. Candidate theses (Phase 2)

Each candidate is tied to ledger entries (01_open_problems.md). Abstracts are written for blind judging: thesis, core argument in three premises, and why a specialist would be surprised. Novelty checks follow in 02_novelty_quick.md.

---

## C01. Computational underdetermination of subsystem structure (I1)

**Thesis.** Whether two quantum systems are independent subsystems in the tensor-product sense or only in the commuting-operator sense is an empirical question, but one that no finite body of evidence can settle. That makes it a new kind of underdetermination, distinct from Duhem-Quine and from observational equivalence.

**Argument.** (1) MIP* = RE implies that the closure of tensor-product correlations is strictly smaller than the commuting-operator set, so the two models are not observationally equivalent. (2) A separating game's commuting value is reached only in the limit, and deciding a game's entangled value is undecidable, so no finite experiment certifies which model obtains. (3) A difference that is empirical in principle yet uncertifiable by any finite record is neither convention nor ordinary underdetermination.

**Why surprising.** Philosophers of physics treat the choice between tensor factorization and algebraic locality as either conventional or settled by relativistic considerations. A theorem in complexity theory shows that it is neither.

## C02. Content without stable vehicles (I2)

**Thesis.** Representational drift shows that stable mental contents are carried by changing neural populations. So no theory that identifies a representation with a fixed neural vehicle, teleosemantics included, can individuate mental representations. Representations are equivalence classes of neural states under a downstream readout, and that readout is part of what a representation is.

**Argument.** (1) In drift studies, the cells encoding a stable task variable change over weeks while behaviour and population readout stay fixed. (2) Teleosemantic and causal theories individuate a representation by the selection or causal history of a vehicle-type. (3) If vehicle-types turn over while content persists, content cannot be fixed by any vehicle-type's history. It must be fixed at the level of the readout map.

**Why surprising.** The argument derives a readout-relative functionalism from a neuroscientific finding, against the dominant naturalistic theories of content.

## C03. Conciliation without peers (I3)

**Thesis.** What makes splitting the difference accuracy-conducive is that the two judgments' errors are partly independent, not that the parties are epistemic peers. The crowd-within evidence shows that one person's repeated judgments meet this condition. So intrapersonal conciliation is rational, and "peerhood", the central concept of the disagreement literature, does no epistemic work.

**Argument.** (1) Averaging two estimates improves expected accuracy exactly when their errors are imperfectly correlated and of comparable variance. (2) Repeated judgments by the same person satisfy this; the improvement is larger with delay. (3) So the condition that matters is error structure, which peerhood neither entails nor is entailed by.

**Why surprising.** The argument relocates the whole peer-disagreement debate onto a measurable statistical property.

## C04. Joint-carving is not clustering (I4)

**Thesis.** No similarity-based account of natural properties can be scale-invariant, rich and consistent all at once. So Lewisian naturalness, if it carves at the joints, cannot be any function of resemblance distances. It must be primitive or non-metric.

**Argument.** (1) Kleinberg's theorem: no clustering function satisfies scale-invariance, richness and consistency together. (2) Each condition corresponds to a desideratum on naturalness: invariance under the choice of units of similarity, the possibility of any partition being natural, and stability under making resemblances more pronounced. (3) So resemblance-based naturalness violates one of these desiderata.

**Why surprising.** It is an impossibility theorem against resemblance nominalism's account of naturalness, from computer science.

## C05. Misleading by design (I5)

**Thesis.** A speaker who commits to a disclosure policy can, using only true statements, move a rational hearer's beliefs to any point that a liar could reach under the hearer's expectations. So the moral asymmetry between lying and misleading cannot rest on epistemic harm to the hearer. It must rest on the speaker's commitments.

**Argument.** (1) Bayesian persuasion characterizes the beliefs reachable by committed truthful disclosure: the concavification of the sender's value. (2) The posterior distributions reachable by lying to a hearer who correctly anticipates the policy are the same Bayes-plausible distributions. (3) So the hearer's resulting epistemic state does not discriminate the two.

**Why surprising.** It undercuts the leading harm-based rationale for treating misleading as less wrong than lying.

## C06. Supervenient but not derivable (I6)

**Thesis.** The undecidability of the spectral gap gives a physical property that is fixed by the microphysics but not derivable from it by any proof. "Strong emergence" should therefore be defined by derivability, not by supervenience. On that definition strong emergence actually occurs, compatibly with physicalism.

**Argument.** (1) Gappedness of the Cubitt-Perez-Garcia-Wolf Hamiltonians supervenes on the local Hamiltonian. (2) For some of them, gappedness is independent of the axioms of mathematics, so it is not derivable. (3) So supervenience without derivability is actual, and the emergence debate's dichotomy is false.

**Why surprising.** The emergence debate assumes that strong emergence requires a failure of supervenience. Here is an actual case without one.

## C07. The tractability argument for moderate credence (I7)

**Thesis.** Bounded agents ought to avoid extreme conditional credences. The reason is not evidential but computational: approximate conditionalization is NP-hard in general and becomes tractable exactly when extreme conditional probabilities are excluded. This is a new argument for a regularity-like norm.

**Argument.** (1) Ought implies can, for computationally bounded agents. (2) Approximate Bayesian updating is intractable in general (Dagum-Luby) but tractable when conditional probabilities are bounded away from 0 and 1. (3) So an agent who is required to conditionalize approximately is required to keep conditional credences away from the extremes.

**Why surprising.** Regularity is usually defended by accuracy or by open-mindedness. Here it follows from complexity theory.

## C08. Conditionalization is not computable (I8)

**Thesis.** There are computable priors and computable evidence streams for which the conditionalized credence is not computable. So "conditionalize" cannot be a norm for computable agents as standardly stated. The correct diachronic norm is relative to a computable topology on evidence.

**Argument.** (1) Ackerman-Freer-Roy: conditional distributions of computable joint distributions can be non-computable. (2) Ought implies can. (3) So conditionalization must be restated, for instance as conditionalization on computably continuous evidence.

**Why surprising.** It is a non-heuristic, theorem-based limit on Bayesian diachronic norms.

## C09. Imprecision and dynamic consistency (I9)

**Thesis.** The debate over sharp versus imprecise credences is settled by a forgotten economic theorem. Dynamic consistency together with consequentialism forces sharp credences. So imprecise-credence theorists must reject consequentialist dynamic choice, and the Elga-style argument is a special case.

**Argument.** (1) Epstein and Le Breton prove that dynamically consistent beliefs must be Bayesian. (2) Their axioms are weaker than those in Elga's sequential-choice argument. (3) So the imprecise theorist must reject one of their axioms, and the paper identifies which one.

**Why surprising.** It reframes a philosophical stalemate as a corollary of a 1993 theorem.

## C10. What accuracy vindicates on infinite spaces (I10)

**Thesis.** Accuracy-dominance arguments extended to infinite opinion sets, by complete-class reasoning, vindicate finitely additive probability but not countable additivity. So the accuracy-first programme cannot support countable additivity.

**Argument.** (1) Accuracy-dominance arguments are instances of admissibility arguments. (2) Complete-class theorems on infinite spaces characterize admissible rules as limits of Bayes rules, and these include merely finitely additive priors. (3) So non-dominance does not exclude merely finitely additive credences.

**Why surprising.** It settles from the accuracy side the countable-additivity debate (de Finetti versus Kolmogorov).

## C11. Bounded depth on both sides (I11)

**Thesis.** The systematicity debate cannot discriminate humans from transformers by appeal to unbounded recursion. Transformers provably fail at unbounded depth, and humans fail at small depth too. The debate's competence-performance distinction is therefore doing illegitimate work. The real question is how error scales with depth, and that is an empirical comparison.

**Argument.** (1) Hahn: fixed-depth self-attention cannot recognize PARITY or Dyck at unbounded length. (2) Human performance on centre-embedding and bracket matching collapses at small depth. (3) Attributing competence to humans but not to transformers needs an independent criterion, and the natural candidates fail.

**Why surprising.** It uses a negative theorem about transformers to defend them against the classical argument.

---

## C12. Self-prediction limits are not free-will evidence (I12)

**Thesis.** Wolpert's theorem shows that self-prediction limits hold for any embedded inference device in any universe, deterministic or not. So arguments from unpredictability to freedom (Popper, MacKay) prove nothing about determinism. Libertarian epistemic arguments collapse.

**Argument.** (1) The limits hold independently of the dynamics. (2) An argument for indeterminism must rest on a premise that fails in some deterministic world. (3) Self-prediction failure is not such a premise.

**Why surprising.** It makes a precise physical theorem bear on a classic free-will argument.

---

## C13. Liquid democracy needs a centre (I13)

**Thesis.** The epistemic case for liquid democracy requires capping the weight of delegates. That is a non-local, centrally administered constraint. So liquid democracy's epistemic credentials depend on giving up the decentralization that motivates it.

**Argument.** (1) Local delegation mechanisms cannot guarantee both positive gain and no harm (Kahng-Mackenzie-Procaccia). (2) A non-local weight cap can. (3) A weight cap requires a central authority that monitors the whole delegation graph, against the decentralist rationale.

**Why surprising.** It turns an algorithmic result into a dilemma for a popular democratic innovation.

## C14. Awareness growth has no conservative rule (A-A1 to A4)

**Thesis.** No rule for revising credences under awareness growth can be both conservative (preserving ratios among old possibilities) and invariant under re-description of the new possibilities. So the search for a Reverse-Bayesian rule is misguided. Awareness growth is a case of permissible, not determined, belief change.

**Argument.** (1) Conservatism fixes the ratios of old possibilities. (2) The weight of a new possibility depends on how it is partitioned. (3) Any rule fixing that weight violates description-invariance.

**Why surprising.** It converts a growing set of counterexamples into an impossibility result.

## C15. Group minds cannot learn (C-A9)

**Thesis.** Dietrich and List's impossibility of dynamically rational judgment aggregation implies that a group agent cannot both aggregate its members' views non-dictatorially and learn coherently from evidence. Group agency therefore requires either dictatorial updating or a premise-based structure. Corporate learning is thus organizationally constrained.

**Argument.** (1) Dynamic rationality: aggregate-then-update equals update-then-aggregate. (2) It is impossible under standard conditions. (3) So group learning requires sacrificing a condition, and each choice has a determinate organizational correlate.

**Why surprising.** It links an impossibility theorem to a substantive claim about institutional design.

## C16. Responsibility gaps are computationally hard to close (I15)

**Thesis.** Deciding whether a decision structure leaves a responsibility gap at higher orders is Π₂d+₁-complete. So no feasible accountability design procedure can guarantee gap-free multi-agent and AI systems. Responsibility gaps are then a structural cost, not a design flaw.

**Argument.** (1) Checking gap closure up to degree d is Π₂d+₁-complete. (2) Institutional design must be feasible to check. (3) So gap-freedom cannot be a feasible design requirement.

**Why surprising.** It applies a complexity result to the responsibility-gap debate.

## C17. Physics with unprovable facts (I6)

**Thesis.** The spectral-gap theorem forces a dilemma. Either physical reality contains facts that no mathematical theory we accept can settle (anti-formalism about physics), or gappedness in those models is indeterminate (metaphysical indeterminacy grounded in logic). Either horn is a substantive metaphysical result from a theorem.

**Argument.** (1) Gappedness is independent of ZFC for some models. (2) The model is fully specified physically. (3) So either the physical fact outruns ZFC, or there is no fact.

**Why surprising.** It is a concrete physical instance of Gödelian independence, with metaphysical consequences.

## C18. Complex numbers are empirically indispensable, conditionally (I16, I1)

**Thesis.** The experimental falsification of real-number quantum theory makes complex numbers empirically indispensable, which is a new and stronger premise for indispensability arguments. But the result holds only given the assumption that independent sources are tensor-product independent. So mathematical indispensability is relative to a contested physical assumption about subsystems.

**Argument.** (1) Real quantum theory is refuted in network scenarios with independent sources. (2) The refutation presupposes tensor-product independence. (3) Under commuting-operator independence the separation may fail. So indispensability is hostage to the subsystem question.

**Why surprising.** It links two importable results into a conditional indispensability thesis.

---

## C19. Old evidence as logical learning (I17, D-A9)

**Thesis.** Logical induction yields a principled solution to the problem of old evidence: old evidence confirms a theory exactly when the agent's logical-inductor prices for the entailment increase. This criterion is computable, and it differs from all counterfactual solutions.

**Argument.** (1) Old evidence is a case of learning a logical relation. (2) Logical induction gives coherent-in-the-limit credences over logical sentences. (3) Confirmation by old evidence equals the increase in the price of the entailment.

**Why surprising.** It brings an AI-safety construction to bear on a classic problem in confirmation theory.

## C20. Induction's undecidable core (I18)

**Thesis.** Since learnability itself can be independent of ZFC (Ben-David et al.), whether induction is possible in a given domain can be undecidable. So the problem of induction has a third form, beyond justification and description: settledness.

**Argument.** (1) Learnability of some classes is independent of ZFC. (2) Whether inductive success is possible in a domain is a learnability question. (3) So it can be formally unsettled.

**Why surprising.** It derives a new form of the problem of induction from set theory.

---

## C21. Willpower without a muscle (I14)

**Thesis.** The multisite failure of ego depletion removes the empirical support for limited-resource models of willpower in action theory. What remains is a purely normative account of resolve, on which resolutions bind by reasons, not by a resource.

**Argument.** (1) Resource models predict depletion. (2) There is no depletion effect (d ≈ 0.06). (3) So resolve is not a resource.

**Why surprising.** It is an empirical correction to an influential philosophical model.

## C22. The persistence of aggregation under indeterminacy (C-A1, C-A3)

**Thesis.** Making betterness indeterminate (vague or incommensurable) does not escape population-ethical impossibility theorems. Every supervaluational precisification of a vague betterness relation inherits an impossibility, so the vagueness strategy only redistributes the cost.

**Argument.** (1) Supervaluation: indeterminate betterness is truth in all admissible precisifications. (2) Each precisification is a determinate axiology and falls under the theorem. (3) So some unattractive conclusion is supertrue, or the conditions are not supertrue, which is a cost.

**Why surprising.** It closes the main escape route that impossibility theorists themselves suggest.

---

## C23. Vehicles of thought in machines (B-C2, I2, I11)

**Thesis.** The externalist argument that language-model words refer (natural histories of text) applies equally to their internal representations. Representational drift shows that biological reference does not need stable vehicles either. So the asymmetry claims against language-model reference that rest on vehicle stability fail.

**Argument.** (1) Reference can be fixed by external history. (2) Language models' tokens have such histories. (3) Vehicle-stability objections fail given drift.

**Why surprising.** It combines a neuroscience finding with metasemantics on AI.

---

## C24. Knowability and physical undecidability (A-A5, I6)

**Thesis.** The spectral-gap theorem provides a concrete truth that is unknowable by any idealized formal agent. This gives the knowability debate (Fitch) a non-paradoxical counterexample to the anti-realist principle, one that does not rest on Fitch's logical trick.

**Argument.** (1) Anti-realism holds that every truth is knowable. (2) If gappedness is determinate, there is a truth that no derivation from accepted axioms can establish. (3) Knowledge of such a truth would require going beyond derivation, which the anti-realist cannot allow.

**Why surprising.** It replaces Fitch's logical construction with a physical example.

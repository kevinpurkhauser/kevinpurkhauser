# Round 4: role C (technical) on draft v5

Report reproduced verbatim from the subagent's hand-back.

Referee report: technical and citation check of "Following Conditionalization: Why Computable Credence Needs Presented Densities"

I read only the submission file. Web access was partial. Searches worked, but direct fetches of arxiv.org, ar5iv, dl.acm.org, cfreer.org and people.math.harvard.edu failed because those hosts are not allowed by the session's network settings. So I could not check AFR's section numbering against the full text.

Summary of the technical core. Lemma 1, the posterior formula, the tent-density arithmetic, the bound in the appendix and the Section 5 calculus all check out. The diagonal argument for Proposition 2 is sound, apart from one fixable gap (point 1). Proposition 3 is a correct and standard application of the AFR dominated-case argument. The main problems are four: one proof gap, an unproved closure claim in F3, a misleading use of AFR in Section 5, and overstatements of scope in the abstract and in Section 5.

POINTS

1. [S] Appendix, "The machine", first bullet: the search does not require K to lie in [0,1]. This is a gap in the proof. Z takes values in [0,1], and correctness is only required for P_Z-almost every z. If the search returns a tau whose interval O, and so K, lies partly or wholly outside [0,1], then U's output on K constrains nothing. If K lies wholly outside, the step "U errs on a set of positive P_Z-measure" fails. The step "The search succeeds" is fine, since a generic z in (0,1) gives a suitable tau. Fix: require K to be a subinterval of [0,1] (or of O intersected with (0,1)) of width r. With that change the rest goes through: an interval of width r contains a dyadic interval of length 2^{-j} whenever 2^{-j} <= r/2. Verdict on the proof: correct once this is repaired.

2. [P] Appendix, "an open interval, the intersection of the balls named by tau". Whether the set of reals with a name extending tau is open or closed depends on the convention for Cauchy names. Under the usual convention |q_i - x| <= 2^{-i}, it is an intersection of closed balls, so a closed interval, and it can be a single point. If the convention is |q_i - q_j| <= 2^{-i} for j > i, the set is smaller still. The argument only needs a nondegenerate rational subinterval K, and the search finds one explicitly, so nothing breaks. State the convention and drop "open". Two more details are verified. Checking that tau extends to a name is decidable, since it is the nonemptiness of a finite intersection of rational intervals. And the claim that U outputs m on every name extending tau, and so for every z in K, is correct for an oracle machine that has read only tau.

3. [P] Appendix: the condition n > t is never used. U's computation is a completed finite computation that depends only on e* and tau, so it makes no difference when M* halts afterwards. Either remove the condition or say what it is for (perhaps a reader worried that U might simulate e* far enough to see M* halt; that worry is idle). The same applies to the sketch in Section 3.2.

4. Verified: the arithmetic of the construction. E_n fixes the n+1 digits in positions n to 2n. That leaves 2^{n-1} free prefixes, each an interval of length 2^{-2n}. Total tent mass is 2^{n-1} * 2^{-(n-1)} = 1. Tent peak height is 2 * 2^{-(n-1)} / 2^{-2n} = 2^{n+2}, so the bound c = 2^{n_M+2} in Proposition 3(a) is right. On the central half of a tent the height is at least 2^{n+1}. The posterior bound gives numerator 2^{-n} * 2^{n+1} = 2 and denominator (1 - 2^{-n}) + 2 < 3, so the posterior is greater than 2/3. The final inequality 2/3 - 1/8 > 1/16 holds. On {0,1} with the discrete metric, the precision-2^{-4} output of a correct U lies within 1/16 of the truth in TV. And P_Z >= (1 - 2^{-n}) Leb on [0,1]. All verified.

5. Verified: Lemma 1. If M has not halted by step k, then n_M > k+2, so w_M < 2^{-k-2}. The TV distance between the mixture and the I = 0 component is w_M, and Prokhorov distance is at most TV, so the total error is at most 2^{-k-1} + 2^{-k-1}. The stated bound of 2^{-k-1} for the omitted component is true, and in fact loose: it is at most 2^{-k-2}. The posterior formula in Section 3.2 is correct. It is continuous and computable in z for each fixed M. Since P_Z is equivalent to Lebesgue measure on [0,1], every version agrees with it almost everywhere, which is what the appendix's contradiction needs.

6. Verified: the recursion-theorem step. Lemma 1 is uniform, so M* can compute a presentation e* of mu_{M*} from its own index, and the dovetailed search is effective. "The search succeeds" is correct: if M* never halted, mu_{M*} would put I = 0 with certainty, and a correct U would, on almost every z and every name, output something within 1/16 of delta_0 after reading finitely many terms. There is no circularity, because the computation found is a real finite computation on the fixed program e*.

7. [P] Section 2, "its output is a version of the conditional distribution at y". A version is a kernel, not a value at a point. Say instead that there is a version kappa such that, for P_Y-almost every y and every name of y, the output is kappa(y). The quantifier order matters here and the intended reading is the one just given.

8. [S] Proposition 3(a) and the contrast drawn in Sections 3.3 and 4.3. The D-presentation in (a) hard-codes n_M, and so the halting time. That is exactly the information Proposition 2 shows cannot be extracted. The paper admits this ("not computable from M"). But then "the same family can be updated by one procedure" (abstract) is true only because each member gets a non-uniform presentation that carries the answer-relevant parameter. By FOLLOW's own standard, a format such as "measure program plus program for the disintegration" would also count as good. Fix: (i) say explicitly that FOLLOW does not single out D; and (ii) foreground case (b). There the presentation (rho = mu_M, q = phi(y - z), bound sup phi) is uniformly computable from M, so (b) gives a genuinely uniform positive result over the very family of Proposition 2. The cost is that (b) changes the evidence, which should also be said. Also, for non-halting M, (a) leaves q(1, .) and c undefined. Specify them, for example q(1, .) = 1 and c = 1.

9. Verified: the Proposition 3 argument. The integrals of bounded computable functions against a computable measure, with the bound given, are computable uniformly. The denominator p(y) = integral of q(h, y) rho(dh) is the density of P_Y with respect to lambda, so it is positive P_Y-almost everywhere. The ratio is then computable relative to y. This is a correct rendering of AFR's dominated or bounded-density corollary.

10. [S] F3, closure claims. (a) "Closed under finite computable mixtures (adjoin the index to H and take the larger bound)" holds only if the components share the reference measure lambda. If they have different lambda_j, re-expressing the densities against a common reference requires Radon-Nikodym derivatives d lambda_j / d lambda-bar. By Hoyrup, Rojas and Weihrauch these are not computable in general, and they need be neither bounded nor continuous. Fix: restrict to a common lambda, or prove closure. (b) "Closed under updating relative to the evidence" is plausible, but the paper does not show it for later observations that are not conditionally independent. Here is one construction that works. Keep rho, and set q_new(h, y2) = q(h, y1, y2)/Z(y1), where Z(y1) is the normalizer. This needs a computable positive rational lower bound on Z(y1) to give the new bound c/Z. Such a bound exists relative to y1 whenever Z(y1) > 0. The paper should state this. (c) The format is defined with a single observation variable Y, so repeated updating needs the multi-observation version spelled out.

11. [S] Section 5, "by AFR there are priors with exact dependence (their Y is a function of the underlying randomness) whose conditional distributions are not computable". This is misleading as support for the claim. Every random variable is a function of the underlying randomness, so that is not exact dependence of the observation on the hypotheses in the paper's sense. In AFR's construction the conditioned-on variable is not a function of the target variable, since it involves independent auxiliary randomness. So AFR's example shows that noncomputability arises without exact dependence on H. The fact that some state with exact dependence cannot be updated is trivial, since any state can be lifted to one over (omega, Y). Fix: drop this as evidence, or restate it accurately.

12. [S] Section 5 and abstract item 3, "no continuous quantity the agent may learn is represented as an exact function of the other quantities". As stated within D it is verified: Y has a bounded density with respect to an atomless lambda given h, so P(Y = g(h) | h) = 0. But the claim is relative to the H/Y split, and the paper's own case (a) shows this. There Z is a hypothesis variable reported exactly, and it is accommodated by removing Z from H. In the same way, a smooth exact dependence Y = g(H) can often be handled by reparametrizing H as (Y, W) and using a density of Y given W, as in the coarea or change-of-variables formula. So the stated "cost" for conservation laws is overstated. It is a constraint on coordinates, not on credal content. Fix: state the claim as relative to the presented split and discuss reparametrization explicitly. The paper's description of D as "the general format known to suffice" also overstates. AFR give other sufficient conditions, for example discrete observations, and the paper itself grants that interval formats exist. Say "the general format considered here".

13. [S] Abstract, "posteriors on ever finer intervals converge almost surely to the exact posterior, though not at any computable rate". For every member of the paper's family the posterior is continuous and computable, so a computable rate exists. Section 6 correctly says "no computable rate of convergence in general". Align the abstract with that. The general claim is plausible but unproved here: a computable almost-everywhere modulus would yield a computable version on a large set, contrary to AFR, modulo layerwise or Schnorr-type subtleties. Either cite a result (for example the Huttegger, Walsh and Zaffora Blando work on effective disintegrations and rates) or give the argument.

14. [P] Section 6, Lévy's upward theorem. This is verified for a fixed filtration, such as the dyadic intervals containing y. For arbitrary "nested intervals shrinking to y" chosen from the name, the needed result is a differentiation theorem for measures on R, not Lévy's theorem. In one dimension it holds (Besicovitch-type differentiation), but cite that instead, or restrict to dyadic intervals.

15. [P] Section 6, "It is also right only if the reported intervals have null boundaries". This mixes correctness with computability. The verified fact is that a computable measure gives lower semicomputable probabilities on open sets and upper semicomputable ones on closed sets, so with atoms at the endpoints the probability of a closed interval need not be computable. That is a computability condition, not a condition for the update being "right". Reword.

16. [P] Section 5, slack example. The bound is correct if it is read as a density with respect to rho_h times Lebesgue measure in s times Lebesgue measure in y, with pi(s) absorbed into q. Written as "the joint density of (s, y_1, ..., y_n) given h", it seems to put s on the observation side. Say how s enters the D-presentation. The computations are verified. The sup over s of e^{-1/s} s^{-n} is attained at s = 1/n and equals (n/e)^n. For pi(s) = 2s, 2s * s^{-1} is bounded but 2s * s^{-2} = 2/s is not. q extends continuously to s = 0. The prior mass of (0, d) is of order d^2 e^{-1/d}, which the text's "of order e^{-1/d}" approximates loosely.

17. [S, citation] "This is AFR's argument for the dominated case (AFR 2019, Section 9.3)". Plausible but unverified, and possibly the wrong pinpoint. My search only confirmed AFR sections 2 to 6 (4: Computable Conditional Probabilities and Distributions; 5: Discontinuous Conditional Distributions; 6: Conditioning is Discontinuous). From memory, the positive results are in a later section, with the dominated or density case and the noisy-observation case in separate subsections. 9.3 may be the noisy case rather than the general dominated one. In the LICS 2011 version the density result is reported as Corollary 35. Check against JACM 66(3) and cite the corollary number. Also check whether AFR's "conditioning is discontinuous" and non-uniformity results already contain something close to Proposition 2. The paper says Proposition 2 is a "close relative" of HRW but does not compare it with AFR Section 6. That comparison bears on originality.

19. [P] Introduction: "no version ... is computable on a set of measure one" is a correct but weak statement of AFR's result, which is stronger. A sharper statement would help readers.

20. [S, scope] Section 4.3: "Proposition 2 shows that such a constraint ... does not make conditionalization followable". This is correct given FOLLOW's third clause and the closure clause. But what Proposition 2 isolates is that the density and its bound cannot be computed from the measure, in the spirit of HRW. That is close to the known fact that the density, with its bound and modulus, is extra information. The paper should say plainly that the mathematical novelty is small and that the contribution is the epistemological framing. The paper partly does this already.

VERDICT: major revision. The mathematics is largely correct. The appendix proof needs the [0,1] repair. The F3 closure claims need either a shared reference measure or proofs. The Section 5 use of AFR is misleading. The abstract and Section 5 overstate the rate claim and the "no exact dependence" claim relative to what is shown, and they ignore reparametrization. The AFR pinpoint citation and the HWZB volume and pages need checking.

Scores: originality 5/10, correctness 6/10, importance 5/10.

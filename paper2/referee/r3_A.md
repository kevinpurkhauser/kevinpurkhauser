# Round 3: role A (specialist) on draft v4

Report reproduced verbatim from the subagent's hand-back.

Referee report on "Following Conditionalization: Computable Agents and Inexact Evidence"

Summary of the submission. The paper builds a uniformly computable family of priors mu_M, mixing a uniform law on Z with a tent density f_n, where the weight 2^{-n} records the halting time of M. Each member has a computable posterior on an exact report Y = Z (Proposition 2 setup). No single oracle procedure is correct on all members (Proposition 2, by the recursion theorem). With independent noise of presented bounded density, or with rational interval reports, one procedure works for all members (Proposition 3). From this and a rule-following principle (FOLLOW), the paper concludes that conditionalization is "followable" only relative to a format, and that an open-ended computable learner must model continuous evidence as inexact. Appendix B adds a non-recognisability result and a Goedel-style corollary.

I checked the mathematics line by line and the references so far as I could. The core construction is sound. Several of the philosophical theses, however, claim more than the theorems give, and one positive claim (N4 for interval reports) is false as stated.

Points

1. [S] The thesis "exactness, not the prior, is the obstacle" is not what Propositions 2 and 3 establish. The variable that changes between Proposition 2 and Proposition 3(a) is not only whether the evidence is exact. In 3(a) the procedure is also handed a density (phi, with its bound). In Proposition 2 it receives only a measure. The paper itself concedes this twice. In Section 5.3: "An exact report of Z can be followed if the prior gives Z a presented bounded density given the other hypotheses. The family {mu_M} violates this only because its densities are not presented uniformly." In F4 it notes that the same noisy credal state can be given as a bare joint measure. In that bare-joint-measure format, noise does not help: the same recursion-theorem trick, applied to a family of noise laws with non-uniformly presented densities, defeats any single procedure. So exactness is neither necessary for failure (noisy joint measures without a presented density fail) nor sufficient for it (exact reports with a presented prior density succeed). What the results actually support is that uniform conditioning needs a presented density of the observation relative to a computable reference measure. Whether that density sits in the channel or in the prior is secondary. The fix is to restate Thesis 1, the abstract, F2/F3 and the Conclusion in those terms, and to drop "Proposition 2 shows that restricting priors cannot be the general repair". Section 5.3 shows that one restriction on priors, namely presented densities, is a general repair.

2. [S] The claim that the "natural response" is aimed at the wrong object is overstated, and so is the defence in 5.3 of placing the slack in the channel. Proposition 2 does refute one version of the natural response: requiring only that each prior have computable conditionals. It does not refute the version that requires priors to be presented with densities, which 5.3 grants works. The asymmetry argument ("for an agent whose priors over its targets are meant to answer only to evidence, the channel is the better place") is undercut by 5.4, which treats the channel model as part of the credal state that "answers to evidence like any other part". Either the channel constraint and the prior constraint are on a par, both being constraints on the joint as 5.3 concedes, or an argument is needed for why constraints on readings are epistemically cheaper than constraints on targets. Supply that argument or weaken the claim.

3. [S] N4 and Lemma 3's "whatever its priors" are false for the interval format. Proposition 3(b) is proved only for mu_M, using the fact that Z has no atoms. N4 extends it to every computable prior: "If its format represents continuous sources ... by interval reports, a single procedure is correct on it, whatever its priors over hypotheses". For a general computable prior, the probability of a rational closed interval is only upper semicomputable and need not be computable. Counterexample: let mu be the sum over e of 2^{-e-1} times the point mass at x_e, where x_e = 1/2 + 2^{-s} if machine e halts at step s and x_e = 1/2 otherwise. Each x_e is computable uniformly, so mu is computable. But mu([0,1/2]) is the sum of 2^{-e-1} over the non-halting e, which is not computable. So even the denominator of ratio conditionalization on J = [0,1/2] is not computable, let alone uniformly. Fixes: restrict interval reports to intervals whose endpoints are null for the agent's prior (with the procedure told this, since nullness is not decidable), or let the endpoints be chosen at random or generically, or require an atomless observation marginal. This also affects the "Finite precision" reply in Section 6, which relies on the interval format working for arbitrary priors and arbitrary received intervals.

4. [S] There is an internal inconsistency about what "inexact" means. In format L the learner still Kolmogorov-conditionalizes on an exact real reading y, given as an oracle stream (Section 2; Proposition 3a is "relative to y"). What is inexact is the relation between reading and hypotheses (Section 5.1: "slack relative to the hypotheses"). Yet the abstract says "Representing evidence as an exact point blocks the rule", and Section 6 says the learner "cannot ... treat the limit of its intervals as an exact point to be conditioned on". The L-learner does exactly that. The fix is to say consistently that the obstacle is modelling the reading as a deterministic function of the hypotheses (more precisely, without a presented density; see point 1), not conditioning on a point.

5. [S] The novelty claim needs positioning against known non-uniformity results. Proposition 2 is in substance a uniform non-computability result for density ratios (Radon-Nikodym derivatives) obtained from measure presentations, where each instance is computable. That is a standard pattern in computable analysis. The closest omitted reference is Hoyrup, Rojas and Weihrauch, "Computability of the Radon-Nikodym derivative", Computability 1(1) (2012) 3-13, which places the Radon-Nikodym operator at the level of EC in the Weihrauch lattice. The paper should cite it and say whether Proposition 2, with its almost-everywhere pointwise notion of correctness, follows from it or differs from it. It should also discuss Weihrauch-degree analyses of conditioning more generally. Without this, the reader cannot tell what is new beyond the philosophical framing. The paper partly concedes this ("The mathematics is elementary"), but then rests its originality on Proposition 2 refuting the natural response, which point 2 qualifies.

6. [S] The Proposition 2 proof mixes two representations of observations. Section 2 gives the observation as "an infinite stream of approximations" (a Cauchy-style name), while the proof treats a finite oracle prefix sigma as fixing binary digits, so that [sigma] is a dyadic cylinder of length 2^{-|sigma|}. Under the binary representation the result holds as written (Z "identified with its binary expansion", with the observation space taken as Cantor space). Under the standard representation, a prefix fixes only a rational interval whose width is unrelated to |sigma|. The argument is easy to repair: choose n so large that 2^{-(n-1)} is below the width of the interval the prefix determines, and the interval then contains whole E_n intervals. The paper should state which representation it uses and make the choice of n match. Two smaller items: E_n is defined only for n >= 2, so w_M must be specified when M halts at step 0 or 1. And the paragraph "The computation is genuine" is unnecessary and slightly confused. U's computation on (e*, sigma) is a fixed finite computation whatever M* later does, so no argument about inner simulations is needed.

I verified the rest of the construction. E_n consists of 2^{n-1} intervals of length 2^{-2n}, with total length 2^{-(n+1)}. Tents of mass 2^{-(n-1)} have peak 2^{n+2}, so f_n >= 2^{n+1} on the central halves. The bound P(I=1 | z) >= 2/3 is correct; the true denominator is (1 - w) + w f, so the bound is in fact slightly better. The choice n >= |sigma| + 2 leaves positions n to 2n free inside [sigma]. Lemma 1's error w_M <= 2^{-(k+1)} < 2^{-k} is correct. Total variation bounds Prokhorov distance, as used.

7. [S] Appendix B does not support the parenthetical claim in 3.3 in the form the reader will take it. In the Appendix B family, the updatable members are exactly the non-halting M, and all of them have the same kernel, delta_0 times nu. So the constant procedure is correct on every updatable member, and FOLLOW is trivially satisfied on this family. The result concerns only recognising updatability, through the undefined notion of a procedure that "commits". Define "commits" (for example, a procedure that outputs a certificate, or that diverges off the updatable set) and state explicitly that the family poses no problem for FOLLOW. The reduction itself is correct: restricting a computable version to I' = 1 and dividing by the known rational w_M gives a computable version of B[X | Y], contrary to AFR, and the complement of the halting set is not c.e. The Goedel corollary is also valid under its assumptions. Note, though, that "able to prove these facts" includes proving the AFR theorem in T, and the use of the second incompleteness theorem needs a standard (Hilbert-Bernays-Loeb) provability predicate, not mere interpretation of arithmetic. The corollary is a routine consequence of the halting reduction and adds little, so consider cutting it.

8. [S] The Dutch book paragraph misapplies Meehan and Zhang (2022). As I understand their paper, it concerns Kolmogorov conditionalizers who plan to give the evidence they learn posterior credence zero (improper versions), and the iff condition, evidential uncertainty, is a property of the agent's prior epistemic situation. It is not secured by choosing a proper version when updating. The paper also says the inexact learner "gives the learned reading probability one". But the procedures defined in Section 2 output posteriors over H only, not over the reading, so propriety with respect to Y is not part of what the procedure delivers. The fix is to state Meehan and Zhang's criterion exactly, show that the L-learner meets it, and extend the output to H together with Y if propriety is to be claimed. The paper's own hedge "as I understand it" signals that this has not been checked.

9. [S] The competence/performance defence of FOLLOW (Section 4.2, "Finitude") cites Kripke 1982 but ignores Kripke's own objection to that move. Kripke argues that idealising to "competence" or to ceteris paribus dispositions presupposes the rule being followed. FOLLOW avoids dispositions by quantifying over procedures, but then it needs an account of why a procedure that is correct on a format, rather than one that is correct on today's input, is the one the agent runs. Otherwise the stored-answer reply is merely asserted. A paragraph addressing this would strengthen the one premise on which, as the paper says (F1), "all the weight rests".

10. [S] The "tail matters" claim in 5.4 is stated too strongly. With pi(s) = 2s, the joint density is unbounded for two readings, so the sufficient condition of Lemma 3 fails. That does not show the posterior is uncomputable or that no uniform procedure exists. Either prove a failure or say only that the sufficient condition fails. Also, the relevant behaviour is near s = 0, not in a "tail". I checked the positive half: phi_s <= (2 pi)^{-1/2} s^{-1}, and the supremum of e^{-1/s} s^{-n} is (n/e)^n, attained at s = 1/n. Read as a density with respect to Lebesgue measure on s, the joint-density clause then applies.

11. [P] Proposition 3(a) also needs the noise bound to be given. "Presented bounded" covers this, but say so in the statement, since integrating against a computable measure needs a known bound.

12. [P] The pointer to AFR (2019) "Corollaries 9.6 and 9.7" could not be confirmed. The noisy-observation results are in Section 9.3 of the JACM version ("Conditioning on noisy observations"), so the numbering is plausible, but cite it by section as well. The claims attributed to AFR in 5.4 (a construction whose conditional distribution is discontinuous on a measure-one set; no uniform approximation by shrinking noise) are consistent with my recollection of AFR, but they should carry exact theorem or remark numbers.

13. [P] Bibliography. I verified Rescorla 2025 as The Review of Symbolic Logic 18(1): 186-212, so complete the page range. Meehan and Zhang 2022, RSL 15(3): 722-757; Huttegger, Walsh and Zaffora Blando 2025, PPR 112(1): 307-324; and Lopez-Wild 2026, Philosophy of Science, doi 10.1017/psa.2026.10273, all check out. Marr is cited in the text but missing from the references. The other entries (AFR 2011 LICS 107-116, AFR 2019 JACM 66(3), Freer and Roy 2010 AISTATS, Garber 1983, Hacking 1967, Kolmogorov 1933, Weihrauch 2000, Mahtani 2024) are correct as far as I know. The remark that regular conditional distributions postdate Kolmogorov 1933 is accurate.

14. [P] "Format" is defined loosely ("a class of presentations together with the way they are given"). Since F5 turns on one credal state appearing in two formats, give a precise definition, for example a represented space of presentations with a map onto credal states.

15. [P] The F4 example (a noisy reading given as a joint measure) is placed in "a format of the first kind" without showing that this format fails. It does fail, because the general joint-measure format contains the exact-report family. But say so, and note that this is also why noise alone does not help (see point 1).

Overall assessment. The construction in Proposition 2 is neat and, apart from the representation detail, correct. The observation that per-prior computability of conditionals does not give a uniform updating procedure is worth making to formal epistemologists. But the headline philosophical claims are not supported as stated. The obstacle the results identify is the lack of a presented density, which the paper's own Section 5.3 concedes can be fixed on the prior side. The interval half of the positive thesis is false for arbitrary computable priors. The technical novelty relative to known non-uniformity results (Hoyrup, Rojas and Weihrauch) is not established. All of this is fixable, but fixing it changes the paper's main claims.

Verdict: major revision.

Scores: originality 4/10; correctness 5/10; importance 4/10.

# Calibration: role B on decoy 1

Report reproduced verbatim from the subagent's hand-back.

Referee report on "Following Conditionalization: Computable Agents and the Noise in Evidence"

Summary. The paper uses the Ackerman, Freer and Roy (AFR) noncomputability result to build a family of priors P_M. P_M can be updated computably exactly when the machine M never halts (Proposition 1). It follows that no sound procedure commits on every updatable presentation (Proposition 1'), and that no consistent theory can certify the prior P_{M_T} (Corollary 1). Adding a rule-following principle, FOLLOW, the paper concludes that whether an agent can follow conditionalization depends on how it represents its prior. It then concludes that an open-ended learner must model continuous evidence as noisy. The technical core is mostly correct and is, as the author says, elementary. The philosophical arguments in Sections 4 and 5 have gaps that the paper does not close. I write as a defender of orthodox Kolmogorov conditionalization with exact evidence, treated as an ideal norm. The paper explicitly sets that view aside, but its conclusions (Sections 5 and 7, and the abstract's "normative reason to treat continuous evidence as inexact") reach back into it.

Points

1. [S] The argument in 4.1 is invalid as stated. The text says "The inference from R1 to R3 to R4 is valid" and that its skeleton "was checked mechanically." R3 concerns which agents *can follow* the rule. R4 concerns which agents are *bound* by it. The only bridge is R2, and R2 runs one way only: able agents are bound. To show that the two agents differ in being bound, you also need the converse: an agent that cannot follow the rule is not bound by it as a followed rule. That is an ought-implies-can premise. R2 does not supply it, and the chain the text lists leaves R2 out entirely. Also, R1 does no inferential work once R3 is a premise. R1 is what justifies R3, so "invalid when R1 is dropped" cannot be right of the argument as written. Fix: add the converse premise explicitly, or restate R4 as a claim about ability to follow, which is weaker. Either remove the claim of a mechanical check or report the formalization.

2. [S] The correctness clause of FOLLOW, and the "ideal reading" in Section 2, are ill-defined at single observations. Section 2 says a transition is correct "if and only if the posterior is a version of the conditional distribution at the observed value." Regular conditional distributions are fixed only up to P_Y-null sets. Here every individual y has P_Y-probability zero, so for any y and any measure m there is a version whose value at y is m. On the paper's own definition every transition is therefore correct, and the ideal reading becomes trivial. The same problem infects FOLLOW's demand that a procedure be "correct, by the rule's lights, on every state." Orthodoxy has a principled answer: correctness belongs to the kernel, not to single transitions. The paper's own definitions in Section 2 and Proposition 1 work at the kernel level, almost everywhere. Fix: define correctness for a procedure as computing a version almost everywhere, or at algorithmically random points as in effective-disintegration work. Then rewrite FOLLOW and the 4.2 discussion of "correct on its current input only by accident" in those terms. Once correctness lives at the kernel level, much of the "lucky guess" rhetoric loses its grip. It survives only if the reference class of priors is fixed, which is point 3.

3. [S] FOLLOW equivocates on its domain, and the R3 witness depends on that equivocation. FOLLOW requires soundness "on every state to which the agent applies it." In 4.1 and 4.2 this becomes soundness on "the presentations it could equally have held" and "the family of presentations the agent might hold." The first is an actual domain. The second is a counterfactual reference class that the paper never specifies. On the actual-domain reading, the second agent's constant procedure is sound on every state it is applied to, so R3 fails. The diachronic defence in 4.2 does not help. After one update the agent's next prior is whatever its own procedure outputs, and that can be the tractable presentation of delta_0 x nu. So the "bad" presentation affects at most the first step. Fix: state FOLLOW with an explicit reference class, and argue for that class without assuming the conclusion.

4. [S] By the paper's own generality standard the first agent cannot follow the rule either, so R3 collapses. The first agent's procedure "reads off nu" and is sound "on all priors of this form," which is a syntactic class picked out to suit the example. Section 4.2 says a follower "must apply its procedure to whatever presentations its own past updates and its other belief-forming processes deliver." Those processes can deliver presentations outside the product-form class, on which the read-off procedure is undefined or wrong. If the reference class is "all presentations the agent might come to hold," then by Proposition 1' and AFR no computable agent follows conditionalization. That is not representation-dependence. It is the plain AFR moral that orthodox ideal theorists already accept: Kolmogorov conditionalization is not computable in general. If the reference class can be drawn narrowly for the first agent, it can be drawn narrowly ({P_{M_ZFC}}) for the second. Fix: give a principled, non-gerrymandered reference class that separates the two agents, or drop R3.

5. [S] The child analogy in 4.2 cuts the other way. The child's procedure is general over the full domain of the addition rule. No computable procedure is general over the full domain of Kolmogorov conditionalization, since all computable priors on continuous evidence is exactly the AFR domain. So the analogy shows at most that conditionalization is not followable in the child's sense by anyone. That supports the orthodox view that it is an ideal norm, not a followed rule. Fix: confront this directly. The paper's real conclusion may be that the "followed reading" is not available for the unrestricted rule. That is a different and less surprising thesis.

6. [S] N3 contradicts the paper's own qualification. N3 says the obstacle "holds whatever format the current state is in." A few lines later the paper grants that an exact report of Z can be followed if the prior gives Z a bounded computable density given the other world-variables, "presented as such." Proposition 1' concerns presentations of the specific form P_M. It does not show that every format fails on exact sources. Fix: restrict N3 to unrestricted presentations of the world prior and drop "whatever format."

7. [S] The world/channel boundary does the work in N5, and drawing it begs the question. Moving the bounded-density "slack" into the channel is said to leave "the prior over the world unrestricted." But the channel model is part of the agent's joint credence over (world, observation). A measured quantity is a world quantity, and the observation can always be counted among the world-variables. Relabel that way, and the channel format forbids exactly what the "world" alternative forbids: an exact functional relation between the observed quantity and the variables of interest. So the claimed difference in cost ("Only the first is available to an agent whose beliefs about the world are to be answerable to evidence alone") depends on the labelling. Fix: give a principled criterion for which variables count as "world," or concede that both options restrict the joint prior equally.

8. [S] The normative conclusion is drawn from a capacity fact, and the paper admits as much. Section 6 concedes that excluding exact channels "is not grounded in evidence" and that agents give them probability zero "for reasons that concern its own capacities." The abstract nonetheless claims "a normative reason to treat continuous evidence as inexact," and Section 7 says exact conditionalization "cannot be the rule a computable learner follows." A reason to give a hypothesis probability zero that is admittedly not evidential is a pragmatic or design reason, not an epistemic norm. The orthodox position, that an agent whose evidence really is an exact real value ought to conditionalize on it, stays untouched. The claim that the excluded hypotheses are ones "no physical measurement is believed to satisfy" is also too quick. It ignores exact evidence that is not physical measurement, such as computed outputs, mathematical quantities, or a simulation's own state variables. Fix: recast the abstract and conclusion as a claim about engineering a followable updater, not about how evidence ought to be modelled.

9. [S] Noisy-channel updating is not conditionalization on the agent's total evidence. On the paper's model the agent receives a full convergent stream of approximations to y, and so learns y in the limit. Conditioning on Y' = Z + E with Y' = y gives a posterior on a different event from the one the agent's evidence settles. The section "The scale is substantive" turns this into a virtue: there is "no computable target to approximate." The orthodox target exists as a measure-theoretic object (a version of the kernel) even when it is not computable. Its non-computability is exactly the AFR fact, not evidence that the channel model is a different and correct norm. AFR themselves note that noise cannot uniformly approximate the exact conditional, and the paper should cite that. Fix: state plainly that the proposal replaces the evidence variable, and argue for that replacement as such.

10. [S] Mathematical error in the "No floor on noise" passage. The text says the mixed noise density "is therefore bounded if and only if E_pi[s] < infinity." The formula just given, and the appendix, make the condition E_pi[1/s] < infinity. With s in (0,1), E_pi[s] is always finite, so the stated condition is vacuous and would wrongly count the uniform prior as fine. The two worked examples are right: density 2s gives E[1/s] = 2 and weight d^2 > 0 on (0,d), and the uniform prior gives E[1/s] infinite. The remark that the results "were checked symbolically" therefore does not cover the biconditional as printed. Fix: replace E_pi[s] with E_pi[1/s].

11. [S] Corollary 2 overgeneralizes. The conclusion "every tractable prior is the limit of intractable ones" is shown only for Q = delta_0 x nu x B_Y. The construction depends on the second component having the same Y-marginal as Q (Lemma 2). For a general tractable prior on (H, Y) you need a further construction, for example taking a product with an AFR pair on an extended space and making sure the observation marginals match. That construction is not given. Fix: either prove the general claim or restrict it. Note too that the topology (total variation) matters and should appear in the statement.

12. [S] Corollary 1 needs more than "enough arithmetic." The step "If it did, it would prove that M_T never halts" requires T to prove Proposition 1, that is, the AFR theorem together with Lemma 2. Robinson arithmetic or a weak fragment does not obviously do that. ZFC does, so the ZFC instance stands. Fix: require T to prove the AFR theorem and Lemma 2 (for instance, T extends ACA_0 or a suitable fragment, or simply T contains ZFC), or state the corollary for ZFC only.

13. [S] Section 1 misattributes a Dutch book result. "Garber (1983) has given Dutch book and converse Dutch book theorems for this rule." This is false. Garber (1983) is the paper on old evidence and logical omniscience in the Minnesota Studies volume. The Dutch book and converse Dutch book theorems for Kolmogorov conditionalization are due to Rescorla (2018, Review of Symbolic Logic 11(4): 705-735), which the reference list contains and Section 6 cites correctly. Fix: replace Garber with Rescorla in Section 1. This should be labelled substantive because the paper leans on these credentials and then, in Section 6, records the Dutch book exposure of channel-modellers only as "an open cost." That cost is serious from the orthodox side. Rescorla's theorem applies when updates depart from every version on a set of positive measure, and a channel-modeller facing truly exact evidence does depart in that way. Leaving it open weakens Section 5.

14. [S] The "Other routes" defence is an argument from ignorance. "Every route for continuous observations in the literature I have been able to consult is dominated" is too thin to carry N5's "only general alternative." AFR also give positive results without a bounded density in some cases, for example conditioning that is continuous or computable on a measure-one set because of special structure. Fix: either survey these results and explain why they do not generalize, or weaken "requires" to "the one known general sufficient condition."

15. [P] The AFR claim "not computable on any B_Y-measure-one set" matches AFR's notion of computability on a measure-one set (their "almost computable"), and I accept it. The gloss "encodes the halting times of all Turing machines" is loose. Please quote the theorem number from the JACM version.

16. [P] I checked that "Corollary 9.7" in AFR is the independent-noise corollary in Section 9.3 of the arXiv version. The general bounded-density result is the preceding theorem and corollary. Lemma 3 and Section 5 cite the special case where they need the general dominated result. Cite the general statement and give version-specific numbering, since arXiv and JACM numbering may differ.

17. [P] Bibliography. The AFR 2017 entry (MSCS 27(8)) is never cited in the text. Lopez-Wild's "Computable Bayesian Epistemology" has now appeared in Philosophy of Science (DOI 10.1017/psa.2026.10273), so "forthcoming, preprint" is out of date. Huttegger, Walsh and Zaffora Blando, PPR 112(1): 307-324, appears with a 2025 date in some listings, so check the year. Rescorla 2018 should have page numbers (705-735). Hacking 1967, Kolmogorov 1933, Freer and Roy 2010, AFR 2011 and 2019 (JACM 66(3), Article 23), and Mahtani 2024 are correct as far as I can verify.

18. [P] Lemma 2, appendix. The restriction argument should say that the restricted finite approximation is renormalized by its actual mass, or else bound the mass error explicitly. As written, "restricts to an approximation of R(lambda) within epsilon/w" passes over the fact that the restricted mass is only within epsilon of w. The fix is routine.

19. [P] Section 4.4 attributes to Garber (1983) a point about representation-dependence among agents with logically equivalent beliefs. That is a fair reading of Garber's logical-learning proposal, but say so precisely, so that it is not confused with the misattribution in point 13.

20. [P] Calling Proposition 1' "no sound general updater" invites a reading stronger than what is proved, which concerns only the uniform family P_M. A one-sentence restatement in the introduction would help.

Overall assessment. The construction is neat and correct up to the repairs above. Proposition 1' and Corollary 1 are reasonable observations, though, as the author concedes, they are a Rice-style corollary of AFR. The two claimed contributions do not yet hold. Representation-dependence rests on a rule-following principle whose domain changes between its statement and its use (points 3 and 4). Under any consistent reading, either both agents follow the rule or neither does. The argument also needs a premise it omits (point 1), and its correctness clause is ill-defined at single observations (point 2). The noise thesis rests on a world/channel boundary that is not principled (point 7). It moves from capacity to norm on grounds the paper admits are non-evidential (point 8). It leaves the Dutch book exposure open (point 13). From the orthodox standpoint, the paper's own results support the conclusion that conditionalization on exact continuous evidence is an ideal norm no computable agent fully follows. The paper's preferred conclusion, that the correct followed norm conditions on inexact evidence, does not follow from them. There are also factual and mathematical errors: the Garber attribution, the E_pi[s] condition, and the overgeneral Corollary 2.

Verdict: major revision.

Scores: originality 6, correctness 4, importance 5.

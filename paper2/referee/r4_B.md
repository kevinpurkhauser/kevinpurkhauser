# Round 4: role B (defender) on draft v5

Report reproduced verbatim from the subagent's hand-back.

REFEREE REPORT: "Following Conditionalization: Why Computable Credence Needs Presented Densities"

Summary of the submission. The paper builds a uniformly computable family of priors mu_M, each a mixture of a uniform law and a "tent comb" density f_{n_M} whose index depends on the halting time of M. Proposition 2 says that each member has a computable posterior, but no single procedure updates every member when members are given only as programs for the joint measure. Proposition 3 says that once a density q and a bound c are supplied, one procedure (AFR's dominated-case argument) updates every presentation. From this, using a rule-following principle (FOLLOW), the paper concludes that the followability of conditionalization depends on how credal states are represented and not on the credence function. It also concludes that, in the density format, no learnable continuous quantity is an exact function of the rest. The paper repeatedly concedes that the ideal reading of conditionalization is untouched.

I checked the mathematics carefully and it is largely correct. My objections are to the philosophical inferences, which in several places beg the question against the orthodox view, and to the novelty and generality claims. I write as a defender of exact Kolmogorov conditionalization as an ideal norm, and of the view that computability constraints, where they apply, attach to credence functions.

POINTS

1. [S] FOLLOW's third clause quantifies over the wrong inputs, and this begs the question. Location: Section 2 (FOLLOW) and Section 4.2 ("The single case"). FOLLOW asks for a single procedure that is correct on every presentation in a format, so the prior becomes an input to the rule. In the paper's own addition analogy, the inputs to a rule are its arguments. The arguments of conditionalization are pieces of evidence, and the rule is applied relative to the agent's prior. A program that computes the kernel y -> P(. | Y = y) for a fixed prior is exactly one procedure that handles every case the rule is applied to, namely every observation y. Section 4.2 calls such a procedure "a stored answer". That description is wrong: the procedure stores a function, not an answer, just as an adding machine stores the addition algorithm and not a table of sums. The single-case objection is therefore not met. It is ruled out by a definition that moves priors into the argument place. Fix: argue independently that the agent's prior belongs in the argument place, for instance by showing that realistic agents must update priors they did not have in advance. Alternatively, restate Proposition 2 as a result about procedures that are uniform over priors, and drop the claim that it bears on whether a given agent follows the rule.

2. [S] The closure clause carries the argument, but the paper never states it precisely. Location: Section 2 ("F is closed under the agent's updating and belief-forming operations") and F2. The step from Proposition 2 to the conclusion requires that the format of some realistic agent contains the whole family {mu_M}. Yet the paper never names a belief-forming operation that takes an agent from a state it holds to an arbitrary mu_M given in the measure format. Updating does not do this, because an agent with prior mu_{M0} only ever holds posteriors of mu_{M0}. Consider instead the format K_{M0}: the posteriors of one prior, each presented together with a program for its own disintegration over the remaining observations. That format is closed under updating, and one procedure (run the stored kernel) is correct on all of it. So FOLLOW, as stated, is met by an agent whose only constraint is on its credence function: a prior with a computable disintegration that the agent can access. The paper anticipates the one-element-format trivialization, but closure under updating alone does not block K_{M0}. If the intended operations include mixing with arbitrary computable priors, the paper must say so, defend it as a norm on agents, and then face point 4. Fix: list the operations explicitly and justify each one. As written, F2 is a conditional whose antecedent is never shown to describe any agent.

3. [S] The contrast between "credence function" and "representation" collapses. Location: Sections 1, 4.3 and the abstract, claim 1. (a) In computable probability, every computability property of a measure is already defined through a representation: Prokhorov names, as in the paper's own Section 2. The literature the paper targets has therefore never treated computability as a representation-free filter on measures. That makes the paper's opponent partly a strawman. (b) "mu has a computable bounded density with respect to rho x lambda" is a property of the credence function, once the reference measures are fixed. Every mu_M has this property. What fails is uniformity, that is, computing the density from an index of the measure. Calling uniformity "representation" rather than "a constraint on a class of credence functions indexed in a certain way" relabels the distinction without establishing it. An orthodox theorist can accept everything the paper proves and say that the agent must have access to its own conditional densities. That is a requirement that the agent can compute something its credence function determines, which is the same kind of requirement as asking the agent to compute its own kernel. Fix: show a case where two agents with the same credence function and the same access to its computable features differ in followability. Proposition 3(a) does not show this, because the D-presentation contains information (the halting time) that the M-presentation does not make available.

4. [S] The density format succeeds by storing the answer, the very thing the paper condemns. Location: Section 3.3 ("It is just not computable from M, because it reveals the halting time") and Section 4.2. In case (a), the D-presentation of mu_M encodes n_M, which is exactly the non-uniform information Proposition 2 diagonalizes against. The paper never explains why it is legitimate to package the halting time in the presentation but illegitimate to package the kernel itself. Fix: give a principled criterion that separates presented densities from presented kernels. Without one, Proposition 3 adds little to the remark that a procedure can compute once it is given the hard part.

5. [S] The claim that "the credence functions are the same throughout" is false for case (b). Location: Section 3.3, last paragraph. In (b), the credal state is a measure on (I, Z, Y) with Y = Z + xi. Only its marginal on (I, Z) is mu_M, and the evidence is a different variable. The claim holds only when Proposition 2 is compared with (a). Fix: confine the claim to (a), and drop (b) as evidence that the obstacle "is not the prior".

6. [S] The density format is not closed under re-splitting, which undercuts F3 and the closure clause. Location: Section 2 ("The split is part of the state") and F3. A learner in format (b) has H = (I, Z) and rho = mu_M given as a measure. Suppose it later learns Z exactly. That requires a density of Z given I, which is f_{n_M}, and this cannot be extracted from rho. So a natural learning sequence (first a noisy reading, then an exact report of a hypothesis variable) takes the agent out of any single correct procedure. The closure properties in F3 (updating, mixtures, adding conditionally independent observations) leave this out. Fix: require that rho itself be given in D-form for every variable that may later be observed, and state the resulting recursive format. That format is more demanding than the paper suggests.

7. [S] The format definition is inconsistent with closure under updating. Location: Section 2 ("finite descriptions") and Section 2, "Updating and correctness" ("I treat the post-update state as a presentation relative to the evidence received"). A post-update state is presented relative to an oracle for y. It is not a finite description, so D as defined is not closed under updating. F3's first closure claim depends on a relativized notion of format that the paper never defines. Fix: define relativized formats and check that FOLLOW and Proposition 3 hold uniformly in the oracle.

8. [S] Claim 3 ("no exact dependence") is false for an equally general sufficient format, and it depends on which variables are chosen. Location: abstract, item 3; Section 5. Suppose (X1, X2) has a computable bounded density p, and Y = X1 + X2 exactly. Reparametrize: (X1, Y) has the computable bounded density p(x1, y - x1), which is a D-presentation with H = X1. The posterior over X2 is the pushforward of the posterior over X1 under x1 -> y - x1, which is computable relative to y and uniformly so. Now take the format D' whose presentations are a D-presentation together with a computable bi-measurable reparametrization (with computable inverse). D' has a single correct procedure, contains D as the case of the identity map, and represents Y explicitly as an exact function of hypothesis variables the agent reasons about. So "in the general format known to suffice, no continuous quantity the agent may learn is represented as an exact function of the other quantities" is either false (D' is at least as general and known to suffice) or true of D only by definition. This also removes the "cost" claimed for conservation laws in Section 5, since linear and smooth invertible constraints are exactly what reparametrization handles. Fix: withdraw claim 3, or restate it as a property of D under a fixed choice of coordinates, and treat the open question in Section 5 as at least partly answered.

9. [S] The paper misdescribes where the obstacle lies: the bound is not what matters. Location: Section 4.3 ("a presented density, with a bound") and Section 3.3(a). In the family, the joint density of (I = 1, Z) with respect to counting measure times Lebesgue is w_M f_{n_M}. Its peak is 2^{-n} * 2^{n+2} = 4 for every M, so a uniform bound exists. What blocks uniform updating is that this function is not uniformly computable in sup norm: the spikes do not shrink as n grows. Also, in case (a), where Y = [0, 1] is compact and q is computable and continuous, the supremum of q is computable, so the given bound c adds nothing. The structure that matters is a computable density, not a bounded one. Fix: correct the emphasis. Also define c for non-halting M, since c = 2^{n_M + 2} is undefined there (any c >= 1 works because rho(I = 1) = 0).

10. [S] The Lévy upward theorem is cited for more than it gives. Location: Section 6, "Finite precision", second reply ("posteriors on nested intervals shrinking to y converge, for almost every y ... (Lévy's upward theorem)"), and the abstract. Lévy's upward theorem covers conditioning along a fixed filtration, for example the dyadic cells containing Y. The intervals an agent receives from an arbitrary Cauchy name do not form a fixed filtration. Convergence along arbitrary shrinking intervals is a differentiation question. For general Borel measures on R, the Besicovitch differentiation theorem gives convergence for centred intervals. Off-centre shrinking intervals can fail without a doubling-type condition. Fix: state the result for a fixed nested partition (Lévy) or for centred intervals (Besicovitch), and say which one the agent's interval updates actually use. This matters to an orthodox reader, because this convergence result is what the paper offers as the way the ideal governs bounded agents.

11. [S] The paper overstates how much it challenges orthodoxy. Location: abstract; Sections 1, 6 ("The evaluative ideal") and 7. The paper concedes that the ideal reading is untouched, that Rescorla's Dutch book theorems apply, and that its conclusion is only conditional (Section 6, "From capacity to norm"). On the orthodox view, conditionalization is a standard and not an algorithm. Ideal norms need not be satisfiable by bounded agents, and any computability constraint is a constraint on admissible credence functions. Nothing in the paper engages this view. Its main thesis is therefore a claim about algorithmic-level cognitive models and probabilistic programming systems. The title and Section 7 ("It is not ... the rule a computable learner follows unless its credences come with densities") read as stronger than the arguments support. Fix: state the scope in the title and the conclusion.

12. [S] The novelty is thin compared with AFR. Location: Section 1 ("What is new is the epistemological reading"). AFR already present the positive results as conditions under which inference is possible: independent noise with a bounded computable density (Corollary 36 in the LICS version), plus the dominated case. They draw the moral that probabilistic programming systems should condition on noisy observations or on presented densities. Proposition 2 is a short diagonal argument of a standard kind in computable analysis, and the paper itself calls it a close relative of Hoyrup, Rojas and Weihrauch. The remaining contribution is FOLLOW together with a relabelling, and points 1 to 4 show that this part is contestable. Fix: engage directly with AFR's own discussion of the noisy case and with the later literature on computable disintegration (for example Ackerman, Freer and Roy, "On computability and disintegration", MSCS), and say exactly what is added.

13. [P] The condition "n > t" is redundant. Location: Section 3.2 sketch ("exceeds the length of the search") and the appendix. M* halts only after its search ends, so its halting step m* is at least t, and n = m* + 2 > t holds automatically. Either remove the condition or explain what it is for.

14. [P] The sketch and the appendix use thresholds that do not match. The sketch speaks of an output "close to I = 0 for certain"; the appendix uses precision 2^{-4} and d(m, delta_0) < 1/8. Use the same constants in both.

15. [P] The correctness definition asks for an output that is "a version of the conditional distribution at y". A version is a kernel and not a value at a point. Say instead that the map from y to the output agrees P_Y-almost everywhere with some version.

16. [P] Section 5, "Uncertainty about the slack": pi(s) appears inside the bound on q, which means the reference measure on the s-coordinate is Lebesgue and not the prior. Say this explicitly. The prior mass of (0, d) is of order d^2 e^{-1/d}, not e^{-1/d}; the paper's "of order" is loose. The supremum computation (n/e)^n is correct for n >= 1 on (0, 1), and so is the remark about pi(s) = 2s.

17. [P] The section numbering of the AFR citation needs checking. "AFR 2019, Section 9.3" for the dominated case could not be confirmed. The LICS version places the noisy-observation result at Corollary 36. Cite by theorem or corollary number.

CHECKS OF FACTUAL, TECHNICAL AND BIBLIOGRAPHIC CLAIMS

Technical, verified as correct.
- E_n is a union of 2^{n-1} intervals of length 2^{-2n}. Positions n to 2n are n + 1 digits, and digits 1 to n-1 are free.
- A tent of mass 2^{-(n-1)} on an interval of length 2^{-2n} has peak 2^{n+2}, so c = 2^{n_M + 2} is right. On the central half the tent is at least 2^{n+1}.
- The posterior bound is correct: w f >= 2, so P(I = 1 | z) >= 2 / (3 - 2^{-n}) > 2/3.
- Lemma 1 is correct. If M has not halted by step k, then w_M <= 2^{-(k+3)}, and total variation dominates Prokhorov distance.
- The dyadic-interval step is correct: an interval of width r contains a dyadic interval of length 2^{-j} whenever 2^{-j} <= r/2.
- The diagonal argument in the appendix is valid.
- Proposition 3 is a correct application of the standard fact that integrals of bounded computable continuous functions against computable measures are computable, uniformly.
- The claim that the probability of a closed interval need not be computable when the prior has atoms is correct (it is only upper semicomputable).

Technical, problematic.
- The Lévy theorem citation (point 10).
- "The credence functions are the same throughout" (point 5).
- Closure of D under updating (points 6 and 7).
- Claim 3 (point 8).

Bibliographic.
- AFR 2011, LICS 2011, pp. 107-116: correct.
- AFR 2019, JACM 66(3), Article 23, pp. 1-40: correct. The section number is unverified (point 17).
- Hoyrup, Rojas and Weihrauch 2012, Computability 1(1): 3-13: correct.
- Rescorla 2018, RSL 11(4): 705-735: consistent with the record.
- Rescorla 2025, "Non-factive Kolmogorov conditionalization", RSL 18(1): correct (online 2023). The description of its content is accurate.
- Lopez-Wild, "Computable Bayesian epistemology", Philosophy of Science, doi 10.1017/psa.2026.10273: the DOI resolves to this title. The preprint states the countable-additivity result for countably generated spaces, so "automatically countably additive" should carry that qualifier.
- Huttegger, Walsh and Zaffora Blando, "Bayesian convergence for computably bounded agents", PPR: the title and authors exist. Volume 112(1) usually corresponds to 2026 issues of PPR, so check the year against the volume.
- Heitjan and Rubin 1991; Hacking 1967; Garber 1983; Kripke 1982; Marr 1982; Weihrauch 2000; Mahtani 2024: all correct.
- "Kolmogorov 1933 gives the measure-theoretic notion; regular versions came later": acceptable.
- Characterizing the cited works as treating computability as a filter on credence functions is a fair description of Lopez-Wild. It is less clearly fair to Huttegger, Walsh and Zaffora Blando. Neither work, as far as I can determine, proposes "the prior should have computable conditional distributions" as a response to AFR. The paper should cite someone who actually holds the "natural response", or present it as a hypothetical position.

VERDICT: major revision.

The formal results are correct and clearly presented. However, the central philosophical inference depends on a version of FOLLOW that puts priors in the argument place of the rule and leaves its closure clause unspecified (points 1 and 2). The contrast between representation and credence function is not established (points 3 and 4). The headline "no exact dependence" claim fails for an obvious generalization of the paper's own format (point 8). And the paper concedes the orthodox ideal without engaging the view that computability constraints are constraints on admissible credence functions.

Scores (1 to 10): originality 4, correctness 6, importance 4.

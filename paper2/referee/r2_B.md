# Round 2: role B (defender) on draft v3

Report reproduced verbatim from the subagent's hand-back.

Referee report on "Following Conditionalization: Computable Agents and the Noise in Evidence"

Summary of the submission. The paper modifies the Ackerman, Freer and Roy (AFR) uncomputable conditional so that a prior P_M is updatable exactly when Turing machine M never halts. From this it gets Proposition 1': on the family {P_M}, no updating procedure is both sound (correct whenever it commits) and complete (commits on every updatable member). It then argues, using two principles (FOLLOW and a restricted ought-implies-can), for two claims. First, whether an agent can follow conditionalization depends on its "representational system". Second, an open-ended learner that leaves its priors over hypotheses unrestricted can follow the rule only if it models its continuous sources as noisy, with a bounded likelihood density. I read the paper as a defender of orthodox Bayesianism: exact Kolmogorov conditionalization is the correct update, and ideal norms need not be satisfiable by bounded agents. The formal core is mostly correct and elementary, and the author says so. The philosophical superstructure has three problems. It reads more into Proposition 1' than the proposition supports. It builds its conclusions into FOLLOW. And it describes the agent's evidence in a way that does not fit the very boundedness it appeals to.

Points

1. [S] The construction does not show failure "on the tractable part" of the domain. Locations: Section 4.2 ("an unrestricted system fails even on the tractable part of its domain"; "cannot update at all on others that are just as tractable") and Section 7 ("no sound general procedure can be complete"). Problem: on the family {P_M} there is a single uniform procedure that is correct on every updatable member. Given M, commit to the program K_M. On precision k and oracle y, K_M runs M for k steps. If M has not halted, K_M outputs the k-th approximation of the constant kernel delta_0 x nu. If M has halted, it outputs anything. When M never halts, K_M computes the correct posterior exactly. When M halts, no computable correct posterior exists, so nothing is lost. The procedure "always commit to K_M" is therefore complete, and it is correct on every presentation where correctness is possible. What it lacks is soundness on the intractable presentations, that is, the ability to abstain exactly on the halting set. So Proposition 1' says that the updatable presentations cannot be computably recognised. It does not say that they cannot be updated by a single procedure. The paper says AFR's uncomputable priors "place no demand on completeness". Yet its soundness clause makes them place a heavy demand: abstention. That asymmetry is unargued, and it does all the work. Fix: either (a) argue directly that a follower must abstain on states it cannot update, rather than return a wrong kernel there, or (b) prove a stronger result: that over the open class of all presentations, no uniform procedure computes a correct version on every updatable presentation. The paper proves neither. As things stand, the open agent can conditionalize correctly on every tractable state in the paper's own witness family.

2. [S] Representation-dependence is built into FOLLOW, so R1 to R5 beg the question. FOLLOW (Section 2) makes followability depend on soundness and completeness over "the class of presentations its belief-forming processes can put it in". Once followability is indexed to that class, it is immediate that two agents with the same credence function but different classes can differ in followability. Proposition 1' only provides an instance. The thesis in Part 2 is a restatement of the premise, not a discovery. The arithmetic analogy in Section 4.2 does not supply independent support. A child adding numerals works in a canonical notation on which addition is total and computable. The fair analogue of the open agent is a child who must "add" numbers given by arbitrary programs, including "the step at which M halts, or 0". It is not obvious that we would say she cannot follow the rule for addition. We would say she cannot always find out what her inputs denote. Fix: give an argument for FOLLOW that does not already presuppose that the domain of the rule is the representational system rather than the set of credal states. Or reframe Part 2 as an explication: "here is a notion of followability on which such-and-such holds". Do not present it as a substantive result.

3. [S] The line between a "representational system" and "a domain chosen to suit one case" is not principled. Section 4.2 rejects a domain "chosen to suit one case". But the channel agent's system is defined by stipulation as exactly the class on which the AFR algorithm runs. Section 2 also says that system includes "acceptance of models from others". So the channel agent must refuse or reformat any P_M-style model it is offered, and the open agent could do the same by running a sound procedure that refuses. The paper needs a criterion on which the channel class counts as a system and "presentations of Q in normal form" does not. Fix: state that criterion and show that the channel class meets it and gerrymandered classes do not.

4. [S] R3 is load-bearing and is not defended. Section 4.1 says "The rest of the section defends R1 and R2." But the first half of R5 ("the first agent is bound") needs R3, the converse of ought-implies-can: "can follow implies is bound". From the orthodox side, R3 together with OIC gives a perverse result. An agent escapes the followed norm by becoming more expressive, that is, by enlarging its representational system to admit arbitrary programs, as the "general-purpose probabilistic reasoner" does. Becoming more open-minded should not release an agent from a rationality requirement. Fix: defend R3, and discuss this bootstrapping-exemption consequence explicitly.

5. [S] The evidence model is inconsistent with the boundedness that motivates the "followed reading". Sections 2 and 5 treat the observation y as an oracle, an infinite stream of approximations. A computable agent following a rule in real time only ever receives finitely many approximations, that is, a rational interval. That is evidence of positive probability, which Section 1 puts outside the paper's scope ("evidence of positive probability, where the ratio formula is computable"). So either the followed rule for a bounded agent is ratio conditionalization on finite-precision events, which is computable, needs no noise model, and makes the noise thesis disappear; or the agent is idealised as receiving infinite streams, in which case the "followed" reading has already given up the boundedness that is meant to set it apart from the ideal reading. The orthodox position handles both: exact Kolmogorov conditionalization is the ideal limit, and finite-precision ratio conditioning is what bounded agents do. Fix: explain why the followed rule should be conditioning on an infinite oracle rather than on what is actually received. Note that AFR's remark that the limit of interval conditioning is not computable is a fact about the ideal, not about the followed rule.

6. [S] Closure under updating (Lemma 3, Section 4.1). The paper says the channel class "is closed under the agent's updating". The posterior after observing y is computable only relative to y. For almost every y (any algorithmically random y), it is not a computable measure, so it is not a presentation in the class defined in Section 2. The representational system must therefore contain oracle-relative presentations. Proposition 1' presumably relativises, but the paper should say so, and say what FOLLOW quantifies over after the first observation. Fix: define the system relative to the accumulated evidence, and check that the channel-agent witness still works.

7. [S] Overreach about Bayesian cognitive science (Sections 2, 5 and 6). The paper says the followed reading "is the one in play when Bayesian models are offered as accounts of how minds or machines learn", and that Bayesian models of cognition "presuppose" open-ended rule-following. Much of that literature is explicitly at Marr's computational level. Its models specify what is computed, not an algorithm the mind follows, and approximate algorithms such as sampling and variational methods are treated as a separate implementation question. On that reading, which is the dominant one, the paper's conclusion has no target. The claimed "independent ground" for noisy likelihoods in perception models (end of Section 5) also inherits point 5. Fix: narrow the claim to algorithmic-level theories that posit exact conditionalization as the executed procedure, and cite some.

8. [S] The "noise" gloss in N5 is too strong. Section 5 says: "A source with a bounded density given the hypotheses is one whose reading is not a function of them. Such a source is noisy." Bounded density with respect to an arbitrary computable reference measure only rules out the reading being a deterministic function of H with non-atomic range. It does not imply measurement noise in any physical or epistemic sense. The slack can just as well come from coarse-graining in the hypothesis space: the agent's H omits variables on which the reading depends. The reference measure can also be singular with respect to Lebesgue measure. Fix: replace "noisy" with "not determined by the modelled hypotheses", and drop the implication that the agent must regard its instruments as imperfect.

9. [S] The "only general alternative" claim is underargued (Section 5, "Other routes"). Any computably recognisable syntactic class of presentations on which conditioning is uniformly computable gives a followable system. Examples are finite mixtures of a fixed computable family, and classes certified by a supplied modulus of continuity of the conditional distribution. Many of these restrict neither the priors in the paper's sense nor the channel. The dichotomy in N5 ("restrict priors or model noise") is therefore not exhaustive, and the author says only that no other route is "known". Fix: say what counts as a "general" route, and test the claim against certificate formats other than a density bound, for example a supplied modulus of continuity for the kernel.

10. [S] The open question about learning the noise scale may have an available answer (end of Section 5). Unboundedness of phi_s(y - h) as s goes to 0 blocks a direct application of the bounded-density theorem. But computability of the posterior over (h, s) needs only that the normalising integral and the likelihood-weighted measures can be computed. With the paper's own prior pi(s) proportional to e^{-1/s}, all negative moments are finite, so the integrated likelihood is bounded. An integrability modulus could plausibly serve as the certificate. Fix: either attempt this, or state precisely which hypothesis of AFR's dominated-case theorem fails.

11. [S] The technical claims I checked hold up, with caveats.
- Lemma 1 is correct. Running M for k steps gives error at most 2^{-(k+1)}.
- Lemma 2 is correct. Both components share the Y-marginal B_Y, so the mixture weights are unchanged by conditioning on Y. The restriction argument works because the slices I = 0 and I = 1 are at distance 1 and epsilon is less than 1/2. Renormalising uses w, which is computable and positive for a fixed halting M. This is a non-uniform step, which is fine for the purpose.
- Proposition 1' is a valid Rice-Shapiro style argument (see point 1 for its import).
- In Corollary 1, Goedel's second theorem needs T to interpret enough arithmetic. Any T that proves Proposition 1 does, but say so. "T does not prove that it does" has an ambiguous "it". The step from "T proves P_{M_T} updatable" to "T proves M_T never halts" uses the halting direction of Proposition 1 inside T, so T must formalise the AFR theorem. That is plausible for ZFC and should be stated.
- Corollary 2 is correct. The TV distance is exactly w, because the components are disjoint in I. But "Rice-Shapiro style reasoning" concerns openness in the topology of the represented space (the weak or Prokhorov topology), not total variation. The inference goes through only because TV convergence implies weak convergence. Say this.
- The noise-scale calculations are correct. The sup of the integrated Gaussian likelihood is attained when every y_i = h. The substitution in the appendix gives the integral from 1 to infinity of t^{n-2} e^{-t} dt, which is finite. For pi(s) = 2s, E[s^{-1}] = 2 and E[s^{-2}] diverges, so it does cover one reading but not two.
- In Lemma 3, "Any prior over hypotheses" is correct but trivial. The channel requirement constrains the joint, as the paper admits in Section 5.

12. [S] Rescorla (2025) is used for more than it shows (Section 6, "The Dutch book"). Rescorla's non-factive theorem concerns conditioning on a proposition that may be false or may have non-zero prior probability. It does not concern replacing an exact-observation model with a noisy likelihood model. It shows that Kolmogorov conditionalization keeps its Dutch book credentials under non-factive learning. It does not show that the channel-modeller's re-specified prior is rationally permissible. Also, from the orthodox side, an agent that adopts a noisy channel model because exact models are not followable, and not because of evidence, has a prior shaped by computational convenience. The paper concedes this ("not grounded in evidence"). Orthodoxy can say this is a departure from the ideal prior, and the paper's ideal/followed split does not answer that. Fix: either argue that the channel model can be evidentially apt, or concede that the noise thesis recommends a known accuracy cost.

13. [P] Citations and attributions I checked.
- AFR 2011 (LICS, 107-116) and AFR 2019 (JACM 66(3), Article 23, 1-40) are correct.
- "Corollary 9.7" as AFR's independent-noise corollary is confirmed in the arXiv version (1005.3014). It follows from Corollary 9.6.
- AFR's remark that conditioning cannot in general be uniformly approximated by shrinking the noise is accurately reported. Their point is that one cannot computably tell how little noise suffices for a given accuracy.
- AFR's abstract speaks of noise with a "sufficiently smooth" bounded density. The paper's "bounded computable density" agrees, since computable functions are continuous. It is worth noting that this excludes uniform noise with discontinuous edges.
- Rescorla 2018 (RSL 11(4): 705-735) is correct.
- Rescorla 2025 (RSL 18(1), DOI S1755020323000345) is correct. It was online in 2023, and the issue starts at p. 186, so add page numbers.
- Huttegger, Walsh and Zaffora Blando, "Bayesian convergence for computably bounded agents", PPR 112(1): 307-324 is consistent with the Crossref listing I found, though one profile still lists it as forthcoming. Check before publication.
- Lopez-Wild, "Computable Bayesian epistemology", Philosophy of Science, DOI 10.1017/psa.2026.10273 is consistent with the records I found. As far as I can see, its main thesis is that there are no computable merely finitely additive measures, and that computable Bayesianism occupies a middle position between ideal and bounded epistemology. The paper's gloss, "computable rationality should constrain priors", fits this.
- Hacking 1967 (Phil Sci 34(4): 311-325), Garber 1983 (MSPS 10, 99-131) and Freer and Roy 2010 (AISTATS) are correct.
- Mahtani (2024) is OUP and is correct. "Argues that credences are hyperintensional" is a loose gloss of a book mainly about how credence ascriptions depend on the objects of credence. Tighten it.

14. [P] Notation. AFR condition the other way round in places, so state the mapping to their variables. "Proposition 1'" is used before Proposition 1 is stated. The abstract's "commits to an update on every such prior" should say "on every such presentation", since the result is about presentations, not measures.

15. [P] Section 4.4 says the gap "is not a shortfall from a uniformly attainable ideal". But the ideal (the kernel) exists uniformly. What fails is uniform attainability, and that holds for logical omniscience too (no consistent computably axiomatised theory decides all of arithmetic). The claimed disanalogy with logical non-omniscience is weaker than presented. The recognition problem in point 1 is structurally the same as the incompleteness of the agent's logic.

Overall assessment. From the orthodox standpoint, the ideal reading of conditionalization survives untouched, and the author concedes this. The followed reading is defined so that its conclusions largely follow from FOLLOW. The headline formal result shows that updatable presentations cannot be computably recognised, not that they cannot be updated (point 1). The noise thesis depends on an infinite-oracle model of evidence, which is in tension with the boundedness the paper invokes (point 5). The mathematics is clean and honestly presented as elementary. The noise-scale observation, that no floor on noise is needed, is a small genuine contribution. But the central philosophical claims need either a stronger theorem or an independent defence of FOLLOW and R3.

Verdict: major revision.

Scores: originality 5, correctness 5 (the formal results are correct, but the interpretive claim that the open agent fails on tractable states is not supported by the construction), importance 4.

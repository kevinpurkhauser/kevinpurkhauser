# Round 4: role D (generalist) on draft v5

Report reproduced verbatim from the subagent's hand-back.

Referee report: "Following Conditionalization: Why Computable Credence Needs Presented Densities"

What the paper adds: It gives a short, essentially correct diagonal construction of a uniformly computable family of priors, each with a computable and even continuous posterior, that no single procedure can update from bare measure presentations. The same family is uniformly updatable once a bounded density is presented, and the paper uses this contrast to argue that, on a "followed" reading of conditionalization, the computability constraint attaches to the format in which credences are held rather than to credence functions. The mathematical core is modest and largely anticipated by the computable analysis and probabilistic programming literatures, which the author partly concedes. The philosophical reframing is interesting, but at present it is stated more strongly than the results support.

Points

1. [S] Class-level constraints on credence functions are not ruled out, so consequence 1 is overstated. Abstract item 1 says "Constraints on credence functions do not make the rule followable", and Section 4.3 says the literature's credence-function constraint "does not make conditionalization followable". Proposition 2 shows only that per-prior constraints fail. In the family, sup f_n = 2^{n+2} and the Lipschitz constant of f_n grow without bound in n. Take instead the class of joint laws whose density of Y given H, relative to (H-marginal) times lambda, is bounded by a fixed C and Lipschitz in y with a fixed constant L. Then a single procedure working only from measure presentations can compute posteriors. The ratio P(g(H); Y in B(y,eps)) / P(Y in B(y,eps)) approximates the Bayes ratio with error of order L eps / p(y). p(y) can be bounded below from approximations, and ball boundaries are null. As far as I can see, this class is closed under updating and under conditionally independent observations from a fixed channel, and it is closed under mixtures. That is a constraint on which credence functions an agent may have, not on how they are presented, and it makes the rule followable in the measure format. Fix: restrict thesis 1 and abstract item 1 to per-prior constraints, or argue that uniform regularity constants are really representational. Either way the paper should discuss this option explicitly.

2. [S] The contrast between Propositions 2 and 3 does not isolate densities. Section 3.3 says the obstacle "is whether the credal state is held together with a density for the observed quantity relative to the rest." For this family, every D-presentation in (a) carries c = 2^{n_M+2} and a program for f_{n_M}, and so reveals the halting time. Any presentation that leaks n_M would work equally well, for instance the measure format plus a natural-number bound, or plus the index n_M. The paper itself notes that the D-presentation "is just not computable from M, because it reveals the halting time." What the construction shows is that non-uniform information must be supplied. It does not show that a density is the information needed. The title ("Needs Presented Densities") and Section 7 therefore overclaim. Fix: either give a necessity result (for example, that any uniformly updatable format closed under the stated operations computably yields bounded densities), or retitle and recast the thesis as "some presented regularity information is needed; bounded densities are one known sufficient form".

3. [S] FOLLOW is underspecified, and this makes the "no exact dependence" thesis fragile. The second clause requires closure under "the agent's updating and belief-forming operations", but those operations are never fixed. Consider a "kernel" format in which a state is presented together with programs for its posteriors after every finite data sequence. One fixed evaluation procedure updates every state in it, it is closed under updating, and it admits exact dependence (H uniform, Y = H, posterior delta_y). It seems to fail closure under mixtures, because re-weighting the components needs Radon-Nikodym derivatives of the component marginals. But it fails only if the agent is required to form mixtures. Without a fixed list of operations, then:
(a) FOLLOW may count lookup-like formats as following the rule, which is the very "stored answer" worry Section 4.2 is meant to rule out;
(b) the claim that D is "the general format known to suffice" is open to counterexamples, and consequence 3 loses its force.
Fix: state the required operations (updating, finite mixtures, conditionally independent products, marginalization) as part of FOLLOW. Then show that the kernel format and other known sufficient formats fail them, or restate consequence 3 relative to that list.

4. [S] The closure claims in F3 are partly inaccurate. Two problems.
(a) Closure under finite mixtures holds only if the components share the reference measure lambda. If lambda_1 and lambda_2 differ, densities relative to their average are bounded by 2 but need not be computable. This is the Hoyrup, Rojas and Weihrauch phenomenon the paper itself cites.
(b) "Multiply the densities and the bounds" fails in the paper's own Section 5 example. When the noise scale s is in H, each factor phi_s is unbounded in s, so the product bound has to be computed for the whole block (the (n/e)^n calculation), not by multiplying per-observation bounds.
Fix: add the common-lambda condition, and state the product rule only for factors that are separately bounded.

5. [S] The convergence claims in the abstract and Section 6 are partly misattributed and partly unproved. Lévy's upward theorem gives almost sure convergence along a fixed filtration, for example dyadic intervals. It does not cover arbitrary "nested intervals shrinking to y" such as those fixed by the agent's incoming approximations. On the real line, symmetric or centred intervals need a differentiation theorem (Lebesgue or Besicovitch), and for general non-centred shrinking sets in higher dimensions the claim can fail. Separately, "though not at any computable rate" (abstract) is asserted but not proved. It plausibly follows from AFR, since a computable almost-everywhere rate would make the conditional computable on a measure-one set. But the sense of "rate" (uniform, or pointwise relative to y) needs stating, and the derivation should be given. Fix: specify the interval scheme, cite the right theorem, and add a short lemma for the rate claim.

6. [S] Overstatement about coarsening at random. Section 6 says interval conditioning is right "only if" the report model is coarsening at random (Heitjan and Rubin 1991). Heitjan and Rubin give CAR (together with distinctness) as a sufficient condition for ignoring the coarsening mechanism. It is not a necessary condition in general. Fix: replace "only if" with "if", or cite a necessity result for the setting at hand.

7. [S] Novelty relative to AFR needs a sharper statement. AFR's JACM paper already discusses the discontinuity and non-uniformity of conditioning, including cases with continuous conditionals. As I recall, it also has positive results for continuous kernels. The paper should say exactly why those results do not already give a uniform procedure for the mu_M family, which they should not, given Proposition 2. Each P_M(I = 1 | Z = z) is continuous, so a reader will ask how this squares with continuity-based positive results. The probabilistic programming literature also already treats exact conditioning on continuous observations as needing likelihoods ("observe" or "score" statements). That supports the thesis but reduces its novelty, and it should be acknowledged. I could not confirm the pinpoint "AFR 2019, Section 9.3" for the dominated-case theorem. Please check it, since my recollection puts the bounded-density positive results in an earlier section.

8. [S] The Section 5 cost argument relies on reparametrization in a way that weakens the stated thesis. The escape "learn one of them only as a function of the others that it has already learned" shows that exact dependence can be kept by a computable change of variables that leaves the dependent quantity unlearned. So the thesis concerns which variables are designated as observations, not which exact relations an agent may believe. The label-free statement in Section 5 should be reworded to make clear that it constrains the observation designation, not the content of the credence function. Otherwise it conflicts with Section 4.3's claim that the content is fully given.

Checks of the technical claims (I verified these and they are correct unless noted)
- E_n: digits n to 2n give n+1 fixed digits and 2^{n-1} intervals of length 2^{-2n}. Tents of mass 2^{-(n-1)} give total mass 1, peak height 2^{n+2} (matching c = 2^{n_M+2}), and height at least 2^{n+1} on the central half. Correct.
- Lemma 1: if M has not halted by step k, then w_M <= 2^{-k-3}, and Prokhorov distance <= total variation. Correct.
- Appendix bound: w f >= 2 and the denominator is below 3, so the posterior is > 2/3; 2/3 - 1/8 > 1/16. Correct. Every interval of width r > 2 * 2^{-(n-1)} contains a dyadic interval of length 2^{-(n-1)}. Correct.
- Proposition 3: integrals of bounded computable functions with a known bound against computable measures are computable, and the denominator is positive P_Y-almost surely. Correct.
- Section 5: sup over s of e^{-1/s} s^{-n} = (n/e)^n, attained at s = 1/n. Correct. With pi(s) = 2s the bound holds for one reading but not two. Correct. The prior mass of (0,d) is about d^2 e^{-1/d}, so "of order e^{-1/d}" is right only up to a polynomial factor.
- A bounded density relative to an atomless lambda implies that Y is not almost surely a function of H. Correct.
- The statement that closed-interval probabilities need not be computable for priors with atoms is correct.

Presentational points
9. [P] The symbol pi is used both for the constant in (2 pi)^{-n/2} and for the prior density on s. Rename one.
10. [P] w_M is undefined for non-halting M. Say that the mixture weight is 0.
11. [P] The condition n > t in the appendix and in the sketch is never used. Drop it, or explain why it is there.
12. [P] Whether the set of reals with a name extending tau is open or closed depends on the convention for Cauchy names (strict versus non-strict bounds). Fix a convention, and note that names with strict slack give a nondegenerate K.
13. [P] Section 3.2 says "at least 2/3" and the appendix says "> 2/3". Make them consistent.
14. [P] The abstract's numbered consequences repeat the introduction almost word for word. Shorten one of them.
15. [P] Section 4.1's F1 to F5 would read better as an explicit premise-conclusion argument with the role of each proposition marked.

Assessment against the criterion. The paper is careful, honest about its scope, and its mathematics is sound. Its contribution to philosophical knowledge is the reframing: under a followed reading, computable rationality constrains representations. As it stands, that reframing is undercut by three things: the unaddressed class-level alternative (point 1), the failure to isolate densities as what matters (point 2), and an underspecified FOLLOW principle (point 3). All three can be repaired, and the repaired paper would make a modest but real contribution.

Verdict: major revision.
Scores: originality 6/10, correctness 6/10, importance 5/10.

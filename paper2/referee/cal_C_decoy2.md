# Calibration: role C on decoy 2

Report reproduced verbatim from the subagent's hand-back.

REFEREE REPORT: "Following Conditionalization: Computable Agents and the Noise in Evidence"

Summary of the submission. The paper builds a uniformly computable family of priors P_M from the Ackerman, Freer and Roy (AFR) noncomputable conditional distribution. A prior in the family is updatable exactly when M never halts. From this it argues (i) that no sound general updater commits on every updatable member of the family (Proposition 1'), (ii) that whether an agent can follow conditionalization depends on how it represents its prior, given a rule-following principle FOLLOW, and (iii) that an open-ended learner can follow conditionalization while leaving its world prior unrestricted only if it models continuous sources as noisy (bounded likelihood densities). The mathematics in Section 3 is mostly sound and elementary, as the author says. The philosophical arguments in Sections 4 and 5 contain a central gap, and there are several technical and citation errors.

POINTS

1. [S] Wrong incompleteness theorem (Corollary 1). The passage reads: "it would prove that M_T never halts, and so its own consistency, contrary to Gödel's first incompleteness theorem." A consistent theory being unable to prove its own consistency is Gödel's SECOND incompleteness theorem. This is a factual error. Fix: replace "first" with "second".

2. [S] Corollary 1 has the wrong hypotheses. It is stated for "any consistent, computably axiomatized theory that interprets enough arithmetic". The step "if T proved that the presented prior has a computable conditional distribution, it would prove that M_T never halts" needs T to prove the contrapositive of Proposition 1: if M halts, P_M has no computable version. That requires T to formalize computable measure theory, Lemma 2 and the AFR noncomputability theorem. A theory like Robinson's Q, or even PA, cannot naturally express or prove these statements about measures and kernels. So "enough arithmetic" is not enough. Fix: state the corollary for theories extending ZFC, or for any consistent, computably axiomatized T that proves Proposition 1 (for instance a suitable second-order arithmetic in which AFR is provable). As restated, for T = ZFC the corollary is verified.

3. [S] Quantifier overreach in Section 4.4, item 2. The paper says: "no amount of logical skill within a consistent computably axiomatized theory closes the gap (Corollary 1)." Corollary 1 has the form: for every T there is a machine M_T such that T does not certify P_{M_T}. It does not have the form: there is a presentation that no theory certifies. The particular gap for P_{M_ZFC} is closed by ZFC + Con(ZFC), which proves that M_ZFC never halts. So the contrast drawn with logical non-omniscience ("not a shortfall from an attainable ideal") does not follow. Any given instance can be closed by a stronger, still consistent theory. What cannot happen is a uniform closure by one theory. Fix: restate the claim with the correct quantifier order and weaken the contrast with Hacking and Garber to match.

4. [S] The witness for R3 fails as FOLLOW is stated. This is the central gap. FOLLOW requires a single procedure that is "correct, by the rule's lights, on every state to which the agent applies it", and the paper stresses that "FOLLOW does not require the agent to prove anything." Under that definition the second agent has a sound procedure. Take the procedure that, given the exact program P_{M_ZFC}, outputs the kernel delta_0 tensor nu; that also handles the obvious presentations of Q-type priors by reading off nu; and that otherwise never commits (or falls back on the channel updater). If ZFC is consistent, this procedure is correct on every input on which it commits, so it is sound in the sense of Proposition 1'. Proposition 1' rules out only a sound procedure that commits on EVERY updatable P_M. It does not rule out one that commits on the agent's actual presentation and its successors. The text's reply, "Nothing available to the agent distinguishes its own case from those", is an epistemic claim about what can be recognised or certified. That is exactly the requirement that FOLLOW disowns. Section 4.2's diachronic argument does not close the gap either. It shows that the procedure must handle the presentations the agent will in fact come to hold. It does not show that those include the whole family {P_M}. So R3 is not established. Fix: either (a) add and defend an explicit premise that the domain of states to which the agent must apply its procedure includes the whole uniformly computable family {P_M}, or some natural class containing it (for example, every presentation its other belief-forming processes could output), and show why hard-coding is not "following"; or (b) admit that the operative notion is soundness the agent can recognise or certify, and then answer the objection in 4.2 directly, not by denying that proof is required. As the paper stands, thesis 2 rests on this gap.

5. [S] The R1 to R4 inference is not valid as stated, and the mechanical-check claim is dubious. R4 concerns being "bound by" conditionalization. R3 concerns being able to follow it. R2 gives only "able implies bound". To conclude that the two agents differ in whether they are bound, one also needs a converse premise (ought implies can: an agent that cannot follow the rule is not bound by it as a followed rule). Without that premise, R4 does not follow from R2 and R3. Also, R1 does no logical work in the inference itself. It is used to argue for R3, not as a premise from which R4 follows. So the reported mechanical result, that the skeleton "becomes invalid when R1 is dropped", is hard to square with the argument as written. Fix: add the ought-implies-can premise explicitly, mark R1 as a premise supporting R3, and either give the formalization or drop the claim of a mechanical check.

6. [S] Misattribution of the additive-noise result (Section 5). The paper says: "the special case of additive independent noise with a bounded computable density is due to Freer and Roy 2010." Wrong. Freer and Roy (2010, AISTATS) is "Posterior distributions are computable from predictive distributions". It is about exchangeable sequences and de Finetti measures, and the paper itself cites it correctly for that in Section 6. The noisy-observation positive result is in Ackerman, Freer and Roy's own LICS 2011 paper and its journal version (JACM 2019). The arXiv abstract (1005.3014) says that conditioning is computable under independent computable noise with a sufficiently smooth bounded density. Fix: credit AFR 2011/2019, and check whether the precise hypotheses there are "bounded computable density" or need smoothness. The arXiv abstract's wording suggests the paper's phrasing may be slightly too weak.

7. [S] The no-floor result conflicts with the closure-under-mixture property and with learning the noise scale. Lemma 3's mixture closure works by putting the mixture index among the world-variables and using "the maximum of the bounds (or a computable uniform bound in the countable case)." In the no-floor example, the noise scale s ranges over (0,1). If s is treated as a world-variable, which an agent that is to "learn" its noise scale from evidence about the channel needs (Section 6, "credences about how noisy its channel is are set by evidence"), then the likelihood p(y | h, s) = phi_s(y - h) has supremum (2 pi)^(-1/2)/s. That is unbounded over s in (0,1) for every prior whose support reaches 0, including the density 2s. Such a model is not a channel presentation. The bounded-density computation in Section 5 is correct only when s is integrated out of the likelihood. But then s is not a world-variable, its posterior is not delivered by the channel procedure, and repeated observations are no longer conditionally independent given h (they are dependent through s). So the closure-under-new-sources clause of Lemma 3 no longer applies. Fix: either restrict the no-floor claim to a single observation with s marginalized out, or give a separate argument (for instance a computability result for the joint posterior over (h, s) under a tail condition) that covers sequential learning of s.

8. [S] Lemma 3, countable mixtures. The claim "Computable mixtures of channel presentations are channel presentations" is false for countable mixtures whose bounds c_i are unbounded. Example: Gaussian channels with scale 1/i. The parenthetical "or a computable uniform bound in the countable case" assumes that such a bound exists. Fix: state closure for finite mixtures, and for countable mixtures only under the explicit hypothesis that sup_i c_i is finite and given.

9. [S] N3 contradicts the first qualification after N5. N3 says the impossibility "holds whatever format the current state is in, because the obstacle lies in the combination of the state with an exact source." The qualification then grants that an exact report of Z can be followed if the prior gives Z a bounded computable density given the other world-variables, "presented as such". So the format does matter. Proposition 1' is proved only for presentations in the P_M form. Fix: restate N3 as "if the agent's states may include presentations of the P_M form, or more generally if the world prior is unrestricted", and drop "whatever format".

10. [S] N5 and the conclusion rest on an argument from ignorance. "The only general alternative known", "every route for continuous observations in the literature I have been able to consult is dominated". The abstract and conclusion state the thesis more strongly ("requires treating continuous evidence as noisy", "cannot be the rule a computable learner follows"). The paper should bring the headline claims into line with what is shown: bounded-density channels are a sufficient general format, and no rival is known. Note also that AFR (2019) and AFR (2017, MSCS, "On computability and disintegration") treat other settings, such as continuous disintegrations and the Weihrauch degree of disintegration. They bear on whether non-dominated routes exist, and the paper should discuss them. AFR 2017 is in the reference list but never cited in the text.

11. [S] The Dutch book reply is incomplete. The paper concedes that a channel-modeller facing exact evidence is Dutch-bookable under Rescorla's theorem. It then leaves open whether a computable bookie can exploit this. Since the paper's normative conclusion ("the rule such a learner follows is conditionalization on evidence modelled as inexact") competes directly with Rescorla's pragmatic vindication, this open question bears on the main claim and is not a side issue. At minimum, the conclusion should be explicitly conditional on it.

12. [P] Kolmogorov (1933) defined conditional probability and expectation with respect to a sigma-algebra. Regular conditional distributions and disintegrations as kernels came later (Doob and others). "Kolmogorov conditionalization" follows Rescorla's usage and is fine. But attributing the regular-kernel formulation to Kolmogorov 1933 is loose.

13. [P] Lemma 2, appendix: the restricted approximation has mass w plus or minus epsilon, so after scaling by 1/w it is not a probability measure. It has to be renormalized, which changes the bound from epsilon/w to roughly 2 epsilon/w. Harmless, but state it. The hypothesis "if w_M > 0" is not needed for the marginal identity P_{M,Y} = B_Y, which holds for all M.

14. [P] The paper says "Both computations were checked symbolically" and that the argument skeleton "was checked mechanically". These remarks add nothing unless the checks are reported. Drop them or give the details.

15. [P] Reference list issues. The Huttegger, Walsh and Zaffora Blando entry gives PPR 112(1): 307-324, 2026. A Crossref listing gives those volume and page numbers but dates them 2025, and Walsh's own page lists the paper as forthcoming. Check the year. The Lopez-Wild entry is "Forthcoming, PhilSci-Archive 23862". The preprint is dated 2024, and a version appears to be published in Philosophy of Science (DOI 10.1017/psa.2026.10273). Update it. Rescorla 2018 lacks pages: 705-735. AFR 2011 lacks pages: 107-116.

CHECK OF CLAIMS (verified / plausible but unverified / wrong)

- Definition of a computable measure via Prokhorov approximations, and of a computable conditional distribution map as computable on a measure-one set: verified, standard, and matches AFR's definitions.
- AFR noncomputability theorem, that computable X, Y exist whose conditional distribution has no version computable on a measure-one set: verified in substance (LICS 2011, pp. 107-116; JACM 66(3), Art. 23, DOI 10.1145/3321699). The arXiv abstract says the conditional distribution encodes the halting problem. The paper's gloss "encodes the halting times of all Turing machines" is plausible but unverified.
- Lemma 1 (uniform computability of P_M): verified. The approximation of w_M within 2^-k is correct, mixtures with computable weights are computable, and the s-m-n uniformity is correct.
- Lemma 2: verified up to the normalization detail in point 13. The kernel identity is correct. Restriction to the clopen set {1} x X is computable because that set is at distance 1 from its complement.
- Proposition 1: verified, given AFR.
- Proposition 1': verified. The commit set is c.e. and would equal the complement of the halting set, which is not c.e. Its philosophical use in Section 4 is a separate matter (point 4).
- Corollary 1: wrong as stated. It cites the first theorem instead of the second (point 1) and its hypotheses are too weak (point 2). With T = ZFC and the second incompleteness theorem it is correct.
- Corollary 2: verified. The total variation distance is at most w, and for every w > 0 the kernel's mass on I = 1 computes w and, via restriction, a version of B[X|Y], so no member is updatable.
- "Instances of the familiar pattern ... semantic property that is not open": plausible (Rice-style). Fine as a remark.
- AFR positive theorem, that a bounded computable likelihood density in the dominated setting gives a computable conditional distribution: plausible but unverified in its exact hypotheses (computability of the reference measure and of the density as a function). The arXiv table of contents confirms a dominated-setting positive result.
- Additive-noise result attributed to Freer and Roy 2010: wrong (point 6).
- Lemma 3: finite mixtures and products of independent bounded likelihoods are verified (the bound c c' is correct). The countable-mixture clause is wrong without an extra hypothesis (point 8).
- Gaussian noise computations: verified. phi_s(e) is at most phi_s(0) = (s sqrt(2 pi))^-1, so the mixture supremum is (2 pi)^(-1/2) E[1/s]. Under density 2s on (0,1), E[1/s] = 2 and P(s < d) = d^2 > 0. Under the uniform prior, E[1/s] diverges. The philosophical use is limited by point 7.
- Rescorla 2018: verified (RSL 11(4): 705-735). The theorem does concern factive Kolmogorov conditionalization. Note that Rescorla's 2025 RSL paper "Non-Factive Kolmogorov Conditionalization" is directly relevant to the Section 6 Dutch book discussion of inexact evidence and should be engaged.
- Freer and Roy 2010 (AISTATS, posterior from predictive for exchangeable sequences): verified as cited in Section 6.
- AFR 2017 (MSCS 27(8): 1287-1314): verified bibliographically. It is never cited in the text.
- Hacking 1967 (Phil. Sci. 34(4): 311-325), Garber 1983 (Minnesota Studies 10, ed. Earman), Kolmogorov 1933 (Springer), Mahtani 2024 (OUP, The Objects of Credence): verified or consistent with my knowledge.
- Huttegger, Walsh and Zaffora Blando: the paper exists, and Crossref gives PPR 112(1): 307-324. The year is uncertain (2025 or 2026). Plausible.
- Lopez-Wild: verified as PhilSci 23862 (2024 preprint). The citation's status is out of date.

OVERALL ASSESSMENT

The construction is neat and correct. Using an extensionally tractable prior with an uncertifiable presentation to separate the credence function from the agent's standing as a rule-follower is a real idea. The noise thesis is an interesting bridge to perceptual modelling. But the paper's main philosophical claim, representation-dependence, does not follow from the principle it states. FOLLOW disowns certification, and without certification a hard-coded sound procedure is available to the second agent. The noise argument overstates what it shows, and its no-floor refinement conflicts with the closure properties it relies on. There are also a misidentified Gödel theorem, under-specified hypotheses for Corollary 1, a quantifier slip, a misattribution, and several incomplete references. These are fixable, but fixing point 4 needs a new premise and a new defence, not a wording change.

Verdict: major revision.

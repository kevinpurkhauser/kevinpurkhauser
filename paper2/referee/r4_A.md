# Round 4: role A (specialist) on draft v5

Report reproduced verbatim from the subagent's hand-back.

Referee report: "Following Conditionalization: Why Computable Credence Needs Presented Densities"

Summary of the submission

The paper builds a uniformly computable family of priors mu_M on {0,1} x [0,1], indexed by Turing machines. Each member has a computable posterior P_M(I=1 | Z=z), yet no single oracle procedure is correct on every member when the members are given only as programs for the joint law (Proposition 2, proved with the recursion theorem). It then notes that AFR's bounded-density result gives a single procedure for a "density format" D, in which each state comes with rho, a density q, a bound c and a reference measure lambda (Proposition 3). From these it argues, using a principle about following rules (FOLLOW), for three claims: (1) constraints on credence functions do not make conditionalization followable, and constraints on representation do; (2) following the rule is relative to format; (3) in the format known to suffice, no learnable quantity is an exact function of the others. The writing is clear and the technical core is mostly sound. The main philosophical conclusion, however, is stated more strongly than the results support, and a natural counterexample to thesis (1) is not addressed.

Checks of the technical claims

I checked the following and found them correct. E_n is a union of 2^{n-1} intervals of length 2^{-2n}, since n+1 digits are fixed and n-1 are free. With tents of mass 2^{-(n-1)} the total mass is 1. The peak height is 2^{n+2}, so c = 2^{n_M+2} is the right bound, and f_n >= 2^{n+1} on the central half. Lemma 1 holds: if M has not halted by step k, the omitted mass is w_M <= 2^{-k-3}, and Prokhorov distance is at most total variation. The posterior formula is correct. The appendix inequality holds: 2/(3 - 2^{-n}) > 2/3. P_Z >= (1 - 2^{-n}) Leb is correct. A width-r interval contains a dyadic interval of length 2^{-j} when 2^{-j} <= r/2. Proposition 3 is a routine consequence of AFR: with a known bound, integrating a bounded computable function against a computable measure is computable, and the denominator vanishes only on a P_Y-null set. The Section 5 calculation is also correct: sup over (0,1) of e^{-1/s} s^{-n} is (n/e)^n, attained at s = 1/n, and pi(s) = 2s gives a bound for one reading but 2/s, which is unbounded, for two.

Points

1. [S] Thesis (1) as stated is refuted by a class-level regularity constraint. The abstract (item 1), Section 4.3 ("Proposition 2 shows that such a constraint ... does not make conditionalization followable") and the Conclusion claim that constraints on credence functions do not make the rule followable. Proposition 2 shows only that a pointwise property does not suffice: each member having some continuous bounded density with computable conditionals. Now take a uniform constraint on credence functions: every admissible prior on {0,1} x [0,1] has conditional densities for Z given I bounded by a fixed C and Lipschitz with a fixed constant L. On that class there is a single correct procedure in the plain measure format. Interval probabilities mu({i} x [z-d, z+d]) are uniformly computable, because the bound C makes the boundaries effectively null. Dividing by 2d recovers w_i f_i(z) with error at most L d, and the ratio can be computed wherever the denominator is positive, which is P_Z-almost everywhere. No density is presented. The class is closed under conditioning on further independent observations with similarly regular likelihoods, so it is plausibly a format satisfying FOLLOW. What fails in Proposition 2 is the lack of a uniform modulus: the f_n have Lipschitz constants of order 2^{3n} and sup bounds 2^{n+2} that grow with n. Fix: restate thesis (1) as "no per-measure property suffices; uniform updating needs uniform moduli of regularity, which can come either from the presentation (the bound c and the computable q in D) or from a fixed class restriction". The paper should then discuss whether the class route counts as a "constraint on credence functions". If it does, the claimed dichotomy between constraining credences and constraining representations collapses into a familiar point about uniformity.

2. [S] The contrast in Section 3.3 is not a contrast in format alone, and the paper should say so up front. In (a), the D-presentation of mu_M encodes n_M through c and q, so from it one can decide whether M halts. D-presentations can be computably translated into M-presentations, but not the reverse. Proposition 3(a) therefore succeeds by giving the procedure strictly more information, information that is not computable from the measure. The paper concedes this in one sentence ("It is just not computable from M, because it reveals the halting time"). It is the crux, and it makes the result an instance of the Radon-Nikodym non-uniformity (Hoyrup, Rojas and Weihrauch) rather than something new. In (b), the D-presentation is uniformly computable from M, but it is a different credal state on (I, Z, Y) with a different evidential situation. So the claim "It is not the prior, since the credence functions are the same throughout" is true for (a) only. For (b), the joint credal state over H x Y is not the same as in Proposition 2. Fix: separate (a) and (b) explicitly. For (a), state that the good format is an informationally richer, non-uniformly obtainable presentation. For (b), state that the positive result needs a change of evidence, not only of representation.

3. [S] The closure clause of FOLLOW does not single out densities, which weakens claims (2) and (3). Consider a "kernel format": a joint law together with a program computing a version of the conditional kernel for each observation variable, relative to the evidence so far. A single procedure (run the kernel) is correct on it. It is closed under updating relative to the evidence, which is the same relativization F3 uses for D. It also admits exact functional dependence, for example H uniform on [0,1]^2 and Y = H_1, whose disintegration is computable. The closure clause, which Section 2 says is what "matters", rules out one-element formats but not this one. It may fail closure under mixtures, but FOLLOW makes closure relative to "the agent's ... belief-forming operations", so for an agent that only updates it qualifies. Fix: either strengthen FOLLOW (for instance, require closure under a stated list of operations such as mixture and marginalization, and argue that these are mandatory), or weaken the conclusions to "D is one followable format".

4. [S] The single-agent case is not addressed. For an agent with one fixed prior whose only operation is updating, the states it can come to hold are that prior's iterated posteriors. Uniform updating over them is just the computability, relative to the evidence, of the prior's iterated conditional distributions, which is a per-prior constraint. The family {mu_M} matters only if the agent's belief-forming operations can generate arbitrary members presented as measures, for example by adopting priors from programs, testimony or model building. The paper needs this premise, stated and defended, before F2 has any bite against the per-prior response it attributes to the literature.

5. [S] Section 5's "no exact dependence" claim is either definitional or overstated. That the observed Y has a bounded density given H is part of the definition of D, so the label-free statement adds nothing beyond that definition. Exact relations among quantities can still be held in D by a change of variables, which the paper's own case (a) permits. Take H = (a, b) with an exact law and the observation a + b: re-split with H' = a and Y = a + b, which is D-presentable whenever a + b has a bounded conditional density given a. Because the split is part of the presentation, the restriction falls on presentations, not on which exact constraints the agent can believe. The conservation-law "cost" paragraph partly concedes this. Fix: restate the claim as "in each D-presentation, the designated observation is not a.s. determined by the designated hypotheses". Then say exactly which credal states admit no D-presentation for a given learnable quantity: roughly, those in which the quantity's conditional law given every computable complementary coordinatization is not dominated with bounded density, as with singular conditional laws. That is the real cost.

6. [S] The proof of Proposition 2 has small gaps that should be closed.
(i) The halting time m* must exceed every precision at which U's found computation evaluated e*. Otherwise, after M* halts, e* can return different approximations and the "fixed finite computation" claim fails. The condition "n > t" (that is, m* > t - 2) secures this only under an unstated convention for counting steps. State the condition directly: m* is larger than every precision k queried of e*.
(ii) For fast Cauchy names, the set of reals with a name extending tau is an intersection of closed balls, not an open interval, and it can be degenerate. The existence step should choose a name of z with slack, for example q_i within 2^{-i-1} of z, so that the compatible set contains a neighbourhood of z. This is possible because correctness is required for every name.
(iii) The recursion-theorem setup should say that M*, while searching, simulates its own first k steps when U queries e* at precision k. This is well defined, but readers will want it stated.
None of these threatens the result.

7. [S] Citation error: the location of AFR's dominated-case result. Section 3.3 says "This is AFR's argument for the dominated case (AFR 2019, Section 9.3)". In the AFR preprint, Section 9.3 is "Conditioning on noisy observations" (Corollary 9.7 and following). That is the source for Proposition 3(b). The general dominated or bounded-density result that Proposition 3 as a whole relies on comes earlier in Section 9; I believe it is the corollary just before, numbered 9.6 in the preprint. Section numbering also differs between the arXiv and JACM versions. Fix: cite the specific theorem or corollary number in the JACM version, cite the dominated-case result for Proposition 3 in general, and cite the noisy-observation corollary for (b).

8. [S] The abstract overstates the claim about rates. It says posteriors on finer intervals converge "though not at any computable rate". For each mu_M in the paper's own family, the posterior is continuous with a computable modulus and the rate is computable. The body (Section 6) correctly says "no computable rate of convergence in general". Fix: align the abstract with the body, and support "in general" with a reference. The relevant literature is Huttegger, Walsh and Zaffora Blando, "Algorithmic randomness, effective disintegrations, and rates of convergence to the truth" (arXiv 2403.19978).

9. [S] The appeal to Lévy's upward theorem is imprecise. Section 6 says "posteriors on nested intervals shrinking to y converge, for almost every y, to a version of the exact posterior (Lévy's upward theorem)". Lévy's theorem covers a fixed filtration, such as the dyadic partitions. Intervals reported by an agent along a name of y need not form one, since they depend on y and on the name. For such intervals the relevant result is a differentiation theorem. Besicovitch's theorem covers centered intervals for an arbitrary Radon P_Y. For non-centered intervals and a general P_Y, convergence can fail without regularity conditions, such as P_Y being dominated by Lebesgue measure with a well-behaved density. Fix: restrict the claim to dyadic or otherwise filtration-generated intervals, or cite the differentiation theorem and state its hypotheses.

10. [S] Section 6 conflates correctness with computability. "It is also right only if the reported intervals have null boundaries under its prior." Whether ratio conditionalization on an interval is correct does not depend on null boundaries. What depends on them is whether the interval's probability is computable, which is the point the next sentence makes. Fix: separate the two conditions.

11. [P] F3 says D is closed under finite computable mixtures by "adjoin the index to H and take the larger bound". This needs a common reference measure lambda. If the components use different lambdas, the mixture needs densities between them. State the restriction. Closure under updating also takes D outside "finite descriptions", since posteriors are presentations relative to an infinite oracle y. Section 2 acknowledges this; the definition of a format should be amended to match.

12. [P] The abstract describes each mu_M as "a mixture of two laws". For non-halting M it is a single law. This is trivial but easily fixed.

13. [P] Section 1 describes AFR's theorem as "no version ... is computable on a set of measure one". That is close to AFR's formulation, but give the exact theorem number and the notion of computability used (computable on a P_Y-measure-one set). AFR also have stronger forms, such as non-computability on sets of large positive measure, which the paper could exploit.

14. [P] Missing literature that bears directly on the thesis.
(a) AFR themselves already draw the moral that practical inference should condition on noisy observations or use densities. The "epistemological reading" overlaps with their own discussion and should engage with it.
(b) Ackerman, Freer and Roy, "On computability and disintegration" (Mathematical Structures in Computer Science; arXiv 1509.02992) treats disintegration relative to maps, which is the exact-dependence case of Section 5.
(c) The probabilistic programming literature, where "observe" or "score" statements require densities and symbolic disintegration builds them (for example Shan and Ramsey 2017, "Exact Bayesian inference by symbolic disintegration"; Staton et al. 2016). This is close to "credal states as generative models with densities".
(d) Rescorla on the Borel-Kolmogorov paradox, since the paper's claim that "the split is part of the state" is a version of the dependence of conditional probability on the choice of observation variable.

15. [P] Section 4.3's contrast with Hacking, Garber and Mahtani is apt, but brief. One more paragraph separating "content is fixed, executability varies" from the hyperintensionality debates would help, especially since point 2 shows that the D-presentation carries non-computable extra information. Arguably it carries more than the content.

16. [P] The defence of FOLLOW against Kripke is quick. The claim that "which procedure it runs is a fact about its architecture" settles the matter only for engineered systems. For human learners the paper defers to empirical work, so FOLLOW's application to minds is left open. Say so explicitly.

Overall assessment

The construction in Proposition 2 is neat and, with the small repairs in point 6, correct. Proposition 3 is AFR's known result. The philosophical contribution rests on reading the contrast between them as showing that computability constraints must attach to representations rather than to credence functions. That reading is undermined in three ways. A uniform class-level regularity constraint on credence functions already yields a single updating procedure in the bare measure format (point 1). The positive case (a) works only because the presentation carries non-computable information (point 2). And FOLLOW does not single out density formats, which also leaves the "no exact dependence" consequence mostly definitional (points 3 and 5). A revised version that recasts the thesis in terms of uniform moduli of regularity, and says where they may come from, could be a useful, modest contribution to the computable Bayesian literature.

Verdict: major revision.

Scores: originality 4/10; correctness 6/10; importance 4/10.

# Round 2: role D (generalist) on draft v3

Report reproduced verbatim from the subagent's hand-back.

**Referee report on "Following Conditionalization: Computable Agents and the Noise in Evidence"**

**Summary of contribution.** The paper shows, by a simple reduction from the halting problem built on the Ackerman, Freer and Roy (AFR) construction, that no updating procedure is both sound and complete on the computable priors whose conditional distributions are computable. It uses this to argue that whether a computable agent can follow conditionalization depends on its representational format, and that an open-ended follower must model continuous sources as noisy, with a bounded likelihood density. The technical core is correct and elementary. The philosophical theses depend heavily on a contestable principle (FOLLOW), and several claims go beyond what the arguments establish.

**Checks of the technical claims**

Before the points, here is what I verified and found correct.

- Lemma 1: the weight w_M is computable uniformly in M, with error below 2^-k.
- Lemma 2: the kernel computation is correct. The renormalisation step works because w = 2^-n is a known rational when M halts, and because the slices I = 0 and I = 1 are at distance 1, so Prokhorov approximations with error under 1/2 cannot move mass between them.
- Proposition 1' is valid. The set of machines on which the procedure commits is computably enumerable, and soundness plus completeness would make it equal to the complement of HALT.
- Corollary 1 is correct for consistent, computably axiomatized theories that prove Proposition 1.
- Corollary 2: the total variation distance is at most w.
- The Gaussian noise-scale calculation is correct. The supremum of the density is (2π)^(-n/2) E[s^-n]. The substitution t = 1/s gives ∫_1^∞ t^(n-2) e^(-t) dt, which is finite. For the prior π(s) = 2s, E[1/s] = 2 and E[1/s^2] diverges, as stated.
- These references are, to my knowledge, correct: Kolmogorov 1933; AFR 2011 (LICS) and 2019 (JACM 66(3), Article 23); Freer and Roy 2010 (AISTATS); Hacking 1967; Garber 1983; Rescorla 2018 (RSL 11(4)).

**Points**

1. [S] **The paper misstates what the two agents have available (Section 4.2).** The passage reads: "It is between two systems that make the same tractable states available, only one of which can be followed." This is false. The channel class contains only joint laws of the form μ(dh)p(y|h)λ(dy) with p bounded. Many tractable joint priors fall outside it. One example is Z uniform on [0,1] with Y = Z observed exactly: the posterior δ_y is trivially computable, but the joint has no bounded density. What is true is narrower: both systems allow every computable prior over the hypothesis variables. Fix: restate the claim in that narrower form, and acknowledge that the channel system excludes tractable joint credal states that the open system contains.

2. [S] **FOLLOW ranks the more capable agent below the less capable one (Sections 4.1 and 4.2).** The open agent can run this procedure: if the input is a channel presentation, apply the AFR algorithm; otherwise search for certificates (for example, proofs in some theory T that M never halts). This procedure is sound. It also updates correctly on every state the channel agent can occupy, and on more. Its only failure is incompleteness on states the channel agent cannot even represent. FOLLOW nonetheless says the channel agent follows conditionalization and the open agent does not. So FOLLOW penalises expressive power rather than any lack of competence.

The reply in 4.2 ("a procedure tailored to P_{M_ZFC} is sound only because it refuses to commit everywhere else") answers only a straw procedure that handles a single case. It does not answer a procedure that commits on a large, computably enumerable set of cases. Fix: either (a) argue directly why the domain of competence must be the whole representational system rather than a sub-format the agent can recognise, or (b) relativise FOLLOW to formats ("the agent follows conditionalization on format F"). Option (b) leaves representation-dependence intact but makes it a fact about formats, not about agents.

3. [S] **The addition analogy undercuts completeness (Section 4.2).** Actual children are not complete on numerals: they fail on numerals too long to process. This is Kripke's finitude point in the rule-following debate, which the paper should engage. So the analogy needs a competence/performance distinction, and the paper must show that in-principle incompleteness (Proposition 1') differs in kind from resource incompleteness. A closer analogy cuts the other way. If numbers were given as arbitrary programs that may or may not halt with a numeral, no one could add "complete on the representations". We would not conclude that such a person cannot follow the addition rule; we would say the format is bad. Fix: engage this case explicitly and defend why a bad format means the agent fails to follow, rather than that the format is unsuitable.

4. [S] **The abstract and introduction claim a necessity the body does not prove.** The abstract says the agent "can follow the rule only if it models each continuous source as having a bounded likelihood density given the hypotheses... or else restricts its priors." The argument N1 to N5 establishes two things only: exact source models with unrestricted priors make following impossible, and bounded densities suffice. Nothing shows that noisy models with unbounded densities, or with non-dominated noise, are unfollowable. The paper's own "Other routes" paragraph concedes that "it does not claim that no other route could exist." Fix: replace "only if" in the abstract and in Section 1 (Part 3) with the weaker statement the body proves. Also note that "noisy" is strictly weaker than "bounded density": bounded density with respect to an atomless reference measure implies the reading is not a function of the hypotheses, but the converse fails.

5. [S] **Learning the noise scale is left open, and this conflicts with Section 5 (and is probably easy to close).** The paper says (a) "Learning about s itself is not covered" and (b) "The agent's model of its channel is part of the content of its credal state, and it answers to evidence about the channel like any other part." These conflict. If s is part of the credal state and the agent never conditionalizes on it, the agent is not conditionalizing its whole prior. The gap is probably closable. With π(s) ∝ e^(-1/s), the joint density of (h, s, y) with respect to μ ⊗ Leb ⊗ Leb is proportional to e^(-1/s) s^(-n) times a bounded function, and that is bounded. Bayes' rule with a bounded computable joint density with respect to a product of computable measures should then give a computable posterior over (h, s), by essentially the AFR argument. Fix: generalise Lemma 3 from bounded likelihoods to bounded joint densities with respect to a product reference measure. That would likely settle the open question for this prior and remove the inconsistency. (With π(s) = 2s and two readings the joint density is proportional to 1/s, so that prior remains genuinely uncovered.)

6. [S] **Corollary 1 is stated too broadly.** It says "such as ZFC or any consistent extension of it." Gödel's second theorem requires the extension to be computably axiomatized. A complete consistent extension of ZFC that is not computably axiomatized can prove the true statement that M_T never halts. Fix: write "any consistent, computably axiomatized extension."

7. [S] **Rescorla (2025) is misapplied (Section 6, "The Dutch book").** Non-factive Kolmogorov conditionalization concerns conditioning on a proposition that may be false. The channel-modeller, as the paper's own "Total evidence" paragraph says, conditions on its reading exactly and takes it as true. The noise lies in the modelled relation between the reading and the hypotheses, not in the evidence itself. So Rescorla 2025 does not "confirm" anything about the channel-modeller. Fix: drop the sentence, or explain how non-factive learning bears on this case. Separately, the claim in Section 1 that Rescorla 2025 "extends them to non-factive learning" seems plausible to me as a description of that paper, but I cannot confirm it from memory.

8. [S] **Representation-dependence is close to definitional given FOLLOW (Section 4.1).** Once FOLLOW makes followability a property of a class of states, it is immediate that agents sharing a current state can differ in followability. The only substantive input is that a class of individually tractable states can lack a uniform procedure, which is Proposition 1'. Combined with the restricted OIC, which is nearly analytic, R5 adds little beyond "an agent that cannot follow a rule is not bound to follow it." Fix: present Part 2 honestly as the conjunction of FOLLOW and Proposition 1'. Put the philosophical weight where it actually lies, on the defence of FOLLOW (see points 2 and 3).

9. [S] **The novelty of Proposition 1' relative to AFR needs checking.** As I recall, AFR (2019) discuss the conditioning operator itself, including its non-computability and discontinuity. A non-uniformity result of this kind may already be implicit in their discussion. The paper rightly calls Proposition 1' elementary. It should say precisely whether AFR state any uniformity or non-uniformity result, so that the claimed contribution (Parts 2 and 3) is cleanly separated.

10. [S] **Closure under updating needs relativisation (Lemma 3).** Posteriors are computable relative to the observation y, which is typically a non-computable real. So the posterior state is a presentation relative to an oracle, not a computable presentation. The claim that the class "is closed under the agent's updating" therefore requires the channel class and FOLLOW to be relativised. AFR's results should relativise uniformly, but this should be stated. A related point: requiring a computable density excludes discontinuous densities such as uniform noise unless the notion of computability is weakened. Say which notion is intended.

11. [P] **Two references need checking.**
    - Huttegger, Walsh and Zaffora Blando, "Bayesian convergence for computably bounded agents," *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 112(1), 2025. PPR publishes two volumes a year, so volume 112 should fall in 2026, not 2025. The year or the volume looks wrong, and I cannot confirm the title. Please verify.
    - Lopez-Wild 2026 and the citation "Corollary 9.7 in the arXiv and journal numbering" for AFR's additive-noise result. I cannot verify either from memory. That the numbering is identical in the arXiv and journal versions should be checked.

12. [P] **Corollary 2 names the wrong theorem.** "Rice-Shapiro style reasoning" is loose. For computable points of computable metric spaces, the relevant result connecting semi-decidability with effective openness is the Kreisel-Lacombe-Shoenfield/Ceitin theorem. Also say which topology is meant: non-openness in total variation implies non-openness in the weak topology, which is the one that matters for presentations.

13. [P] **The presentation definition is too narrow.** "Rational points" should be the ideal points of the computable metric space; for Cantor space, for example, they are not rationals.

14. [P] **The examples of excluded hypotheses are too quick (Section 6, "From capacity to norm").** The claim that excluded exact relations are ones "no physical measurement is believed to satisfy" needs examples and argument. The status of computed or simulated readings deserves more than one sentence.

15. [P] **The literature on hyperintensionality is only mentioned.** In Section 4.4, Mahtani's view is summarised in one line, and the hyperintensionality literature is touched on rather than engaged. Contrast 1 in that section is the more useful one and should be developed.

**Overall assessment.** By the competition criterion, the paper makes a modest but real contribution. It identifies a precise, elementary obstacle to uniform computable conditioning even on tractable priors, and it draws an interesting, though overstated, moral about modelling evidence as noisy. The mathematics is largely sound, apart from the scope error in Corollary 1. The philosophical conclusions rest on FOLLOW, whose defence does not yet answer the strongest objection: FOLLOW rates a strictly more capable agent as unable to follow the rule (point 2). The necessity claim in the abstract is not proved (point 4). The noise-scale section is internally inconsistent and probably fixable (point 5).

**Verdict:** major revision.

**Scores:** originality 6, correctness 6, importance 5.

# Round 5: role B (defender) on draft v6

Report reproduced verbatim from the subagent's hand-back.

REFEREE REPORT: "Following Conditionalization: Computability Constrains Spaces of Credences, Not Credences"

Summary. The paper builds a uniformly computable family {mu_M} of priors on {0,1} x [0,1]. Each member has a continuous computable posterior on an exact observation of Z, yet no single oracle procedure, given measure presentations, updates every member (Proposition 2). It then gives three conditions under which one procedure does work: presented density data (3a), a noisy observation channel (3b), and fixed density and Lipschitz constants (Prop. 4). From these it concludes that computability constrains "spaces of credal states, not credence functions," and that admissibility of a credence is "not intrinsic." I write as a defender of orthodox Bayesianism: Kolmogorov conditionalization, ideal norms that bounded agents need not be able to meet, and computability constraints, where they matter at all, applied to individual credence functions. I checked the mathematics line by line and it is essentially correct. The philosophical conclusion does not follow from it. G4 is built into the definition of FOLLOW, and two of the paper's own three positive routes are constraints on individual credence functions.

Verification of the technical claims (all checked by hand)
- E_n: the digits in positions n to 2n are fixed, and digits 1 to n-1 are free. That gives 2^{n-1} intervals of length 2^{-2n}. Correct.
- A tent of mass 2^{-(n-1)} on a base of width 2^{-2n} has peak 2*2^{-(n-1)}*2^{2n} = 2^{n+2}. It is at least half the peak, 2^{n+1}, on the central half. Correct.
- Lemma 1: if M has not halted by step k, then n >= k+3, so the omitted weight is at most 2^{-k-3}. Prokhorov distance is at most total variation, so the total error is at most 2^{-k-1} + 2^{-k-3} < 2^{-k}. Correct.
- The posterior formula is correct, since the Z-density under A is 1. For each fixed M it is continuous and computable. Correct.
- Appendix: the recursion-theorem construction is valid. Self-simulation causes no regress, because the search enumerates finite runs of U on e*, and each such run is a genuine computation whose result is fixed whatever M* later does. The condition "every point of K has a name extending tau" is decidable. If the width-r interval K satisfies 2^{-(n-1)} < r/2, it contains a dyadic interval of length 2^{-(n-1)}, and that interval contains an interval of E_n. The bound P(I=1|z) >= 2/(3 - 2^{-n}) > 2/3 is correct. An error of more than 2/3 - 1/8 exceeds the 1/16 precision. Positive Lebesgue measure gives positive P_Z measure, and this defeats every version of the kernel. I find no error.
- Prop. 4 sketch: the boundary-mass bound 4C*delta (two endpoints, each with a 2*delta neighbourhood of density at most C) and the Lipschitz averaging bounds are correct, apart from truncation at the endpoints of [0,1]. That is harmless but should be mentioned.
- Lévy's upward theorem along the dyadic filtration gives almost-sure convergence to a version of the conditional expectation given sigma(Y). Correct. The remark that interval probabilities are computable when boundaries are null and can fail to be when there are atoms is correct.
- Bibliography: I confirmed Rescorla 2018 (RSL 11(4) 705-735), Rescorla "Non-factive Kolmogorov conditionalization" (RSL, first online 2023, issue 18(1) in 2025; the citation could note the 2023 online date), Huttegger, Walsh and Zaffora Blando, PPR 112(1) 307-324, and Lopez-Wild, Phil. Sci., doi 10.1017/psa.2026.10273. On Lopez-Wild, the "countably generated" restriction matches the preprint, but the journal abstract states the result more generally. The author should check which wording the published version uses. AFR 2011 LICS and 2019 JACM 66(3) Art. 23, Hoyrup-Rojas-Weihrauch 2012, Heitjan-Rubin 1991, Hacking 1967, Garber 1983, Shan-Ramsey POPL 2017, Marr, Kripke, Weihrauch and Mahtani 2024 all match my knowledge. I could not verify two things: that AFR's dominated-case argument is in "Section 9" of the JACM version, and the exact wording "not computable on any set of measure one" for AFR's main theorem. The author should check both against the published text.

Points

1. [S] FOLLOW makes G4 true by definition. FOLLOW (Sec. 2) says a computable agent follows conditionalization only if a single procedure is correct on every reachable updatable state. G4 says whether an agent follows the rule depends on which states it can reach. G4 follows from the definition alone, and the propositions only show that the dependence is not empty. So the "thesis" is a stipulation about the word "follow" plus a non-uniformity example. The dispute with the orthodox view is about rationality norms on credences, and FOLLOW assumes the answer to that dispute rather than arguing for it. Fix: argue independently that FOLLOW, rather than a per-state condition, is the right account of what conditionalization asks of a computable agent. Otherwise restate the thesis as a claim about rule-following capacity, not about "rational credence."

2. [S] Prop. 4 and route 3(b) are per-credence constraints, which refutes the slogan. Sec. 4.3 says the class restriction "is a constraint on credence functions, but on all of them at once. It cannot be stated for one prior in isolation." This is false. "mu has a density q with respect to rho ⊗ Leb such that q <= C and q is L-Lipschitz in y" is an intrinsic property of mu, once C and L are fixed. The norm "every credence function must be (C,L)-regular" applies to each credence one at a time, just as "credences must be countably additive" does. Route 3(b) is likewise intrinsic to each credence: it is a fact about the credence's own likelihood for the reading Y, namely that Y = Z + xi with a bounded computable noise density. So the paper's own positive results show what the orthodox view needs: a stronger per-credence condition with fixed parameters, in place of bare computability of the kernel. Fix: drop "not credences" from the title and abstract, and drop "admissibility is not intrinsic." What the results support is that bare computability of each kernel is too weak a per-credence condition, and that uniform parameters do the work.

3. [S] The "holism about rational credence" goes beyond the hedges. Sec. 4.3 says admissibility "depends on the other credence functions it could have held" and calls this "holism about rational credence." Sec. 5 concedes that "nothing here tells an ideal reasoner what to believe" and that the evaluative ideal is untouched. These two claims conflict. The relational fact the paper shows concerns the agent (does it follow a rule?), not the credence (is it rational?). It is the trivial modal character of rule-following: whether a machine that outputs 5 on (2,3) is adding depends on its other outputs. Fix: remove the claim about rational credence, or give an argument that following the rule is a necessary condition of rational credence. Such an argument has to deal with the orthodox denial that ought implies can for ideal norms.

4. [S] Proposition 2 is a known non-uniformity phenomenon, and the novelty claim is overstated. The construction gives measures mu_M that converge to A in the weak topology while their posteriors do not converge. So the result is the discontinuity of the conditioning operator restricted to measures with continuous posteriors. That is the content of AFR's "conditioning is discontinuous" results, and it is a standard pattern in computable analysis: each instance computable, the operator not uniformly so. The paper calls itself a "close relative," but it then presents the epistemological reading as new ("a kind of holism ... new to this debate"). The paper itself concedes that this "resembles the dependence of computability on representation familiar from computable analysis." Fix: state exactly what Prop. 2 adds beyond the AFR discontinuity results. If the answer is only the recursion-theorem packaging and the "tents plus uniform" family, say so, and lower the novelty claim.

5. [S] The result depends on the representation, not on the space of credences. The procedure gets credal states as Prokhorov presentations. These carry exactly the weak-topology information on which conditioning is discontinuous. With a presentation that includes the density together with a modulus, one procedure handles every mu_M, as 3(a) shows. So "spaces of credences" should read "a chosen representation of credences." The orthodox view can hold that a computable agent's credal state is its credence function together with the information a computable agent must have in order to use it. That is still a per-state requirement. The reply in Sec. 4.2 ("a stored kernel is not closed under (X)") shows only that one particular format fails. It does not show that every per-state format fails. Fix: explain why Prokhorov presentations are the right individuation of credal states for this debate, and consider a per-state format of density plus modulus.

6. [S] Route 3(a) is mislabelled. It presents the full conditional density q, not "regularity data." Once q is given, the update is just Bayes's rule with densities. In Prop. 2's family, q itself is not uniformly computable from M: the I=1 term can reach height about 4 at an arbitrarily late halting time, so no tail bound is available. That supports point 5: the work is done by what the presentation contains, not by any feature of the space. Fix: call it "presented density," and drop the unifying slogan "uniform regularity" for 3(a).

7. [S] Route 3(b) does not update on the exact observation. The abstract and G3 list the regular source model as a source of uniformity that yields a single procedure. But in 3(b) the agent conditions on Y = Z + xi, a different event under a different joint measure. Conditioning on exact Z = z is still not followable for that family. So Sec. 4.3's claim that this route gives a "computational reason ... to model continuous evidence sources as regular channels" recommends changing the evidence model in order to get a computable update. For an orthodox Bayesian, misdescribing what one learned is a departure from conditionalization on one's actual evidence, not a way of following it. Fix: say plainly that 3(b) changes the learning problem, and remove it from the list of ways to follow exact conditionalization.

8. [S] The Dutch book paragraph omits the consequence that favours the orthodox view. Sec. 5 notes that agents on the three routes escape Rescorla's theorems. But Prop. 2 implies that any adopting and mixing agent with a fixed procedure U reaches some mu_M on which U is wrong on a set of positive P_Z measure. By Rescorla's converse Dutch book theorem (2018), such an agent is Dutch-bookable in that state. That is a per-credence diagnosis: the fault lies in a particular state and its update, which is exactly how the orthodox view locates it. Fix: discuss this, and explain why it does not show that the per-credence evaluative framework already captures what matters.

9. [S] The per-prior response is a straw position. The paper admits it attributes the view to no one. No author in computable probability has said that pointwise computability gives uniform computability; the uniform/non-uniform distinction is basic in the field. The orthodox position I defend says computability constraints, where relevant, attach to individual credence functions. It is not refuted by showing that a weak pointwise condition does not give a uniform procedure, because the orthodox can adopt the intrinsic (C,L) condition of point 2. Fix: engage a version of the per-prior view that someone actually holds, or one strengthened in this way.

10. [S] Operation (X) carries much of the weight and is not defended. The family is reachable "by operation (X) alone" only through countable mixtures whose weight sequence p_n(M) hides a halting time. Finite mixtures with known components and weights, which is the model-averaging practice cited as motivation, do not produce the problem. Fix: motivate countable computable mixtures with undecidable support as a real cognitive or engineering operation, or say plainly that the result covers only agents with that capacity.

11. [P] Inconsistent parametrization in Sec. 4.1. Prop. 4 defines q as a density with respect to rho ⊗ Leb, where rho is the H-marginal. In that parametrization q(1,·) = f_n, with sup 2^{n+2} and Lipschitz constant 2^{3n+3}. The text instead cites the Lipschitz constant of 2^{-n} f_n, which is about 2^{2n+3}. That is the density with respect to counting measure ⊗ Leb. Both diverge, so the claim stands, but pick one parametrization. Also note that the marginal density of Z is uniformly bounded by 5 across the family. This deserves mention, because it shows that a density bound alone does not suffice and the Lipschitz modulus is essential.

12. [P] The definition of correctness should say whether U receives the presentation as an index or as an oracle sequence. The appendix uses the index e* through the recursion theorem.

13. [P] In Prop. 3(a), say that q must be normalized so that q rho ⊗ lambda is a probability measure. Explain why lambda must be atomless.

14. [P] The Kripke paragraph is too quick. Saying that "which procedure runs is fixed by its code" for engineered systems does not answer the finitist worry. It moves the question to the program, whose behaviour on unreached inputs is exactly what is at issue.

15. [P] The relation of Prop. 4 to AFR's positive results, and to known results on computing conditional densities with a modulus, should be stated, so readers can tell what is new.

Verdict: major revision. The mathematics is correct and neatly presented. However, the central thesis is either true by the stipulation in FOLLOW (point 1) or contradicted by the paper's own positive results (point 2). The claim about rational credence conflicts with the paper's own hedges (point 3), and the technical core is a repackaging of known discontinuity of conditioning (point 4). A revised version would need to restate the thesis as one about uniform rule-following capacity relative to a representation, and drop the claim about rational credence.

Scores: originality 4, correctness 6 (the mathematics deserves 9; the philosophical inferences in G4 and Sec. 4.3 do not hold), importance 4.

# Calibration: role A on decoy 1

Report reproduced verbatim from the subagent's hand-back.

Referee report on "Following Conditionalization: Computable Agents and the Noise in Evidence"

Summary. The paper takes the Ackerman, Freer and Roy (AFR) theorem that some computable priors have noncomputable conditional distributions and adds a Rice-style family P_M. P_M has a computable conditional distribution exactly when machine M never halts. From this the paper derives three things: no sound general updater (Proposition 1'), a Gödelian corollary (Corollary 1), and a fragility corollary (Corollary 2). It then argues that, given a rule-following principle (FOLLOW), whether an agent can follow conditionalization depends on how it represents its prior. Finally it argues that an open-ended learner must model continuous evidence as noisy. The mathematics in Section 3 and Appendix A is mostly right and is honestly called elementary. The philosophical framing (rule followed versus evaluative ideal, representation-dependence, a computational reason for noise models) is interesting and fairly well executed. But there is one clear misattribution, one false mathematical statement, an invalid argument as written, and several overstatements. I found no fabricated references.

Points

1. [S] Misattribution of the Dutch book theorem (Section 1). The paper says: "Garber (1983) has given Dutch book and converse Dutch book theorems for this rule". This is false. Garber 1983 is the paper on old evidence and logical omniscience. It has nothing on Kolmogorov conditionalization. The Dutch book and converse Dutch book theorems for Kolmogorov conditionalization are Rescorla 2018, Review of Symbolic Logic 11(4): 705-735, and the paper itself credits "Rescorla's theorem" in Section 6. Fix: credit Rescorla 2018 in Section 1, and add the page range to the reference.

2. [S] False boundedness criterion (Section 5, "No floor on noise"). The paper says the mixed Gaussian density "is therefore bounded if and only if E_π[s] < ∞". This is wrong. On (0,1), E_π[s] is at most 1 for every π, so the stated condition always holds. The paper's own uniform-prior example, where the density is unbounded, refutes it. The correct condition, which follows from the paper's own formula sup = (2π)^{-1/2} E_π[1/s] and from Appendix A, is E_π[1/s] < ∞. The two worked examples are correct: density 2s gives E[1/s] = 2 and gives (0,d) mass d^2 > 0, and the uniform prior gives E[1/s] = ∞. The parenthetical claim that the result was "checked symbolically" did not catch the wrong statement, so it should go. Fix: replace E_π[s] by E_π[1/s].

3. [S] The no-floor construction conflicts with the closure and learning claims (Sections 5, 6 and Lemma 3). The bounded mixed density is obtained by integrating s out of the likelihood. The scale s is then not a world-variable, so the agent has no posterior over s. That contradicts Section 6, which says "the agent's credences about how noisy its channel is are set by evidence". Suppose instead that s is put among the world-variables, as the closure-under-mixture recipe in Lemma 3 prescribes ("adjoin the index to the world-variables", bound = max or sup of c_i). Then p(y | h, s) = φ_s(y - h) has no uniform bound as s goes to 0, and the result is not a channel presentation. Integrating s out also breaks the closure-under-new-sources step. Two sources that share an unknown scale are not conditionally independent given the world once s is marginalised, so the product bound in Lemma 3 does not apply. Fix: either (a) show that the AFR dominated theorem still applies when the joint density over (s, world, y) is unbounded but integrable in a suitable computable sense, or (b) admit that learning the noise scale with no floor and the channel format with a uniform bound are in tension, and weaken Section 6 to match.

4. [S] The R1 to R4 argument is invalid as stated (Section 4.1). R2 says only that able agents are bound. R3 gives two agents with the same credence function, only the first of whom can follow the rule. From R2 and R3 we get that the first agent is bound. Nothing yet follows about the second, so R4 ("whether an agent is bound ... is not determined by its credence function alone") does not follow. R4 also needs the converse premise, an ought-implies-can principle: agents who cannot follow the rule are not bound by it as a followed rule. Also, R1 does not enter as a separate premise of the deduction. It is what supports R3. So the claim that the "first-order skeleton was checked mechanically, together with the check that it becomes invalid when R1 is dropped" shows that the formalisation is wrong. The paper should not rely on it. Fix: add the ought-implies-can premise explicitly, state that R1 is the support for R3, and drop the claim of a mechanical check. Alternatively, state the conclusion directly in terms of ability rather than being bound.

5. [S] FOLLOW is equivocated in the witness for R3 (Sections 2 and 4.1-4.2). FOLLOW as stated requires correctness "on every state to which the agent applies it", that is, sound on its domain. The second agent applies the constant procedure only to its one state, P_{M_ZFC}, and there it is correct. The witness works only because Section 4.1 quietly shifts to a modal domain: "the family of presentations the agent might hold", "presentations it could equally have held". That is a much stronger principle. It is also unclear why the agent's possible states should include every P_M, since no belief-forming process in the paper produces them. The diachronic argument in Section 4.2 does not close the gap. The posteriors the agent will have to update are outputs of its own procedure, and it can emit them in any certified format it likes. Only the initial presentation is outside its control. Fix: state FOLLOW in its modal form, defend that form, and say which counterfactual presentations count. One well-motivated option is the family closed under the agent's own belief-forming operations. Then show that this family contains a non-updatable sub-family of P_M type. Without this, R3 is not established.

6. [S] Corollary 1 needs a stronger hypothesis and is overstated in Section 4.4. (a) The step "if T proved the prior updatable, T would prove M_T never halts" requires T to prove the halting direction of Proposition 1, and so the AFR theorem. "Interprets enough arithmetic" (say, Robinson Q) does not ensure this, or even that T can express Prokhorov-computable kernels. Fix: assume that T proves the AFR theorem and Lemma 2, as ZFC does. Some subsystem such as ACA_0 might also do, but the paper would need to show it. (b) Section 4.4 says "no amount of logical skill within a consistent computably axiomatized theory closes the gap". Per instance this is false: ZFC + Con(ZFC) proves that P_{M_ZFC} can be updated. What no single theory can do is certify every case, and that uniform claim is Proposition 1', not Corollary 1. Fix: state the per-theory form correctly and rest the "no attainable ideal" claim on Proposition 1'. Also rename "no theory certifies", since every instance is certified by some theory.

7. [S] Corollary 2 is over-generalised. The text shows only that the particular Q is a total-variation limit of non-updatable priors on an enlarged space with an extra I coordinate. It then concludes "every tractable prior is the limit of intractable ones". As stated this is false. On finite or discrete outcome spaces every computable prior can be updated. On a fixed continuous space the paper gives no argument that an arbitrary updatable P can be perturbed into a non-updatable one: once marginals differ, the mixture's kernel reweights by densities, and Lemma 2 does not apply. Fix: restrict the claim to Q, or prove it for spaces rich enough to embed the AFR pair with matched marginals. Also say that w must be a computable real (as in Lemma 1), or the perturbed priors are not computable at all.

8. [S] N3 says too much (Section 5). N3 says that the impossibility "holds whatever format the current state is in". The first qualification in the same section then concedes that an exact report of Z can be followed if the prior gives Z a bounded density, presented as such, which is a format for the current state. Fix: state N3 as "whatever format, provided the world prior is unrestricted".

9. [S] "The current posterior is a computable measure" (N4) is false in general. The observation y arrives as a stream of approximations and need not be a computable point. The AFR positive result gives a computable map from y to the posterior on a measure-one set. So the posterior is computable relative to y, not computable outright. The iterated-update claim in Lemma 3 therefore needs relativised (oracle) computability, uniformly in the bounds. It also needs the posterior to be well defined at the actual y, which requires positive marginal density there. Fix: restate N4 and the closure-under-new-sources claim in relativised and uniform form, and check the positivity condition.

10. [S] The channel format moves the certification problem rather than removing it. Whether a program computes a density bounded by c is itself a co-c.e. (Π^0_1) property, not decidable. So the channel format works only because the bound is supplied as data, just as the evidence-first format works because the kernel is supplied as data. The real asymmetry the paper needs is that a certificate for a source model can be checked or built locally, without reference to the world prior. That is plausible, but it should be argued for, not presented as the format "reading off" goodness. Relatedly, "places no constraint on what the agent believes about the world, other than about its own evidential channels" does real work, because channels are part of the world. Section 6 concedes that exact deterministic channels get probability zero. So the contrast between constraining beliefs about the world (the rejected option) and constraining beliefs about channels (the accepted one) is weaker than Section 5 claims, and "only the first is available to an agent whose beliefs about the world are to be answerable to evidence alone" needs to be qualified.

11. [S] The claim that every continuous route is dominated may be false (Sections 5 and 6). The paper says the Freer and Roy (2010) exchangeable-sequence route "concern[s] discrete-valued sequences". As I understand it, that result rests on the computable de Finetti theorem, which covers real-valued exchangeable sequences. AFR's JACM paper describes the exchangeable result as covering cases "often where no conditional density exists". If so, it is a non-dominated route for continuous observations, which contradicts "Every route for continuous observations in the literature ... is dominated". I could not check the exact scope of the 2010 statement, so the author must. Fix: check Freer and Roy 2010 and Freer and Roy's computable de Finetti papers, and either engage the exchangeable route as a rival to the noise conclusion or justify putting it out of scope.

12. [S] Novelty and prior literature. AFR themselves state the noise result as their positive answer: computable conditioning holds under independent computable noise with a bounded density. In the arXiv numbering that is Corollary 9.7, which I could confirm. They also note that conditioning on continuous observations cannot in general be approximated uniformly through noise, because one cannot compute a noise scale σ from a target error. That overlaps substantially with the paper's point that "the scale is substantive, not an approximation", and should be acknowledged. Also, Rescorla, "Non-factive Kolmogorov conditionalization" (Review of Symbolic Logic 18(1), 2025), treats Kolmogorov conditionalization on inexact evidence. It bears directly on the noise thesis and on the open "Dutch book" cost in Section 6, but it is not cited. Fix: cite both, and say what goes beyond them, namely the rule-following framing and the representation-dependence thesis.

13. [P] The citation "(the special case of additive independent noise with a bounded computable density is their Corollary 9.7)" matches the arXiv numbering. Earlier versions number it differently, for example Corollary 36. Give the version or the JACM theorem number.

14. [P] Appendix, Lemma 2, restriction step. The Prokhorov approximation λ' of κ'(y) restricted to {1}×X is a sub-probability measure with mass w ± ε. Scaling by 1/w does not give a probability measure, so it must be renormalised. The error bound is then roughly a small constant times ε/w, not ε/w. The argument survives, but the bound as stated is not exact. Also say which product metric is used, so that the two I-slices really are at distance 1.

15. [P] Lemma 2's displayed equation writes (δ_0⊗ν)(A) where ν(A_0) is meant. Kolmogorov 1933 is fine for conditional expectation, but regular conditional distributions, and the disintegration terminology, come from later work (Doob, and the disintegration literature). The paper should not imply they are in the Grundbegriffe.

16. [P] Section 3 says the results are instances of the pattern that "a semantic property of programs that is not open cannot be recognised". Cite the Rice-Shapiro theorem and its represented-space analogue, under which semi-decidable extensional properties of computable points are effectively open. Corollary 2 is then just the observation that updatability is not open.

17. [P] Other references, as far as I could check them, are accurate. AFR 2011 (LICS), AFR 2017 (MSCS 27(8): 1287-1314) and AFR 2019 (JACM 66(3), Article 23) are correct. Huttegger, Walsh and Zaffora Blando, "Bayesian convergence for computably bounded agents", PPR 112(1): 307-324, exists: it was published online in 2025 and the issue may be dated 2026. Lopez-Wild, PhilSci-Archive 23862 ("Computable Bayesian Epistemology", 2024 preprint) exists. Hacking 1967 (Philosophy of Science 34(4): 311-325) and Mahtani 2024 (OUP) are correct. Add page numbers for Garber 1983 (pp. 99-131) and Rescorla 2018 (pp. 705-735).

18. [P] The intro promises results that "should surprise in two directions". Given point 12, temper that. The bullet-heavy layout of the argument sections would read better as continuous prose.

Overall assessment. The core idea is worth publishing. A Rice-style index-set argument shows that computable updatability is a property of presentations, not of credence functions, and the paper uses this to motivate noise models. But several stated claims are false or unsupported as written: the Garber attribution, the E[s] criterion, the general fragility claim, the per-theory reading of Corollary 1, "whatever format", and "the posterior is computable". The central philosophical argument is also invalid without an added premise, and it turns on an unacknowledged strengthening of FOLLOW. The noise conclusion has a tension (point 3) and a possible counterexample route (point 11) that must be resolved. Overlap with AFR's own remarks on noise and with Rescorla 2025 reduces the novelty.

Verdict: major revision.

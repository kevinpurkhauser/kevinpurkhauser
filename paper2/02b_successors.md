# 02b. Successor theses (second cycle)

The first kill round tested ten candidates: nine were killed and one was damaged. Each refuter proposed a narrower thesis that could survive its own objections. Those proposals, revised to answer the objections, are the six successors below. Each abstract states what it concedes to the refuter.

---

## S1. Mathematics gates evidence (from C01)

**Thesis.** After MIP* = RE, whether an experiment bears on the choice between tensor-product and commuting-operator models of independent subsystems depends on mathematical facts: certified upper bounds on a game's tensor-product value. No uniform method supplies these facts. So the empirical content of a physical hypothesis can be real and yet open to evidence only piecemeal, game by game, as proofs arrive. "Empirical content" thus has an access condition that standard accounts of underdetermination and testability omit.

**Argument.** (1) The tensor-product closure and the commuting-operator set are distinct closed convex sets, so some finite Bell functional separates them with a positive gap. (2) To use any particular game as a test, one needs a proven upper bound on its tensor-product value. Bounding such values is not uniformly possible (MIP* = RE; MIP^co = coRE). (3) So which experiments are evidentially relevant to a hypothesis is not settled by the hypothesis and the data. It depends on a mathematical knowledge state that has no uniform completion.

**Concessions.** This is not underdetermination by data. Once a bound is proven, ordinary Bell-type statistics decide. The thesis concerns access to empirical content, not its existence.

**Why surprising.** Accounts of testability (hypothetico-deductive, Bayesian, severity) assume we can compute what a hypothesis predicts for a given experiment. Here that assumption fails in a precise and principled way.

## S2. Why undecidability cannot ground emergence (from C06)

**Thesis.** Undecidability results in physics (Gu, Weedbrook, Perales and Nielsen; Cubitt, Perez-Garcia and Wolf) cannot ground strong emergence. The recent argument that they do (Murialdo, Céspedes and Cifuentes 2025) fails for three structural reasons.
- **Theory-relativity:** each independence result is relative to a theory and removable in a stronger one.
- **Quantifier order:** for each theory some Hamiltonian is independent of it, but no Hamiltonian is independent of all theories.
- **Level-neutrality:** the non-derivability is arithmetical incompleteness encoded in a lattice, so it carries no micro-to-macro structure.

**Argument.**
(1) Strong emergence requires a failure of deducibility from the base that holds because of the base-to-higher-level relation.
(2) In the undecidability constructions, the gap property is equivalent to an arithmetical sentence whose truth value is fixed by the base together with arithmetic. Whatever non-derivability there is attaches to that sentence, not to the passage between levels.
(3) So the results show computational or proof-theoretic limits that are level-neutral, not emergence.

**Concessions.** This is a negative and diagnostic paper. It must go beyond Humphreys (2015) and Ippoliti and Caprara (2021), which it does through the quantifier-order and level-neutrality diagnosis.

**Why surprising.** It separates incompleteness from emergence with a precise test: whether the non-derivability survives replacing the physics by its arithmetical encoding. That test applies to any future undecidability-based argument for emergence.

## S3. The chaining dilemma for resemblance nominalism (from C04)

**Thesis.** Suppose naturalness is to be built from resemblance as a graded, nested structure. Carlsson and Mémoli's uniqueness theorem then forces single linkage as the only method satisfying the hierarchical analogues of Kleinberg's axioms. Single linkage chains objects together through intermediaries, and that chaining is a precise formal version of Goodman's imperfect-community and companionship problems. So the resemblance nominalist must either reject a specific axiom, and say which, or accept that degrees of naturalness are generated by chaining.

**Argument.**
(1) A resemblance-based account of graded naturalness is a hierarchical clustering method on the resemblance structure.
(2) Carlsson and Mémoli: under permutation invariance, a representation condition and a non-expansiveness (Lipschitz) condition, single linkage is the unique such method.
(3) Single linkage groups any two items connected by a chain of close resemblances, however dissimilar the endpoints. That is the imperfect-community phenomenon.

**Concessions.** Naturalness is not a partition, and Lewis and Sider already take it as primitive. The target is the resemblance nominalist (Rodriguez-Pereyra) and conceptual-space accounts, not Lewis.

**Why surprising.** A classic informal objection becomes a theorem-backed dilemma with a precise menu of exits.

## S4. What teleosemantics selects under drift (from C02)

**Thesis.** Representational drift involves co-drift: neural codes change while downstream readouts are continually re-adapted. Teleosemantics must therefore place the selected or stabilized item at the producer-consumer coupling relation, not at the vehicle. That relocation has costs. It strains reproductively established families (Millikan) and makes content depend on an ongoing stabilization process. It thereby moves teleosemantics toward Shea's learning-based stabilization, and away from purely etiological content.

**Argument.**
(1) Neither the encoding population nor the readout is stable over weeks. What persists is the successful coupling, which is maintained by corrective plasticity.
(2) Etiological content needs some item with a selection or stabilization history.
(3) The only item with such a history across drift is the coupling. So content is fixed by the history of a relation, not of a vehicle type.

**Concessions.** This does not refute teleosemantics. Consumer semantics already individuates content partly by use. The thesis is that drift forces a particular choice between versions of it.

**Why surprising.** An empirical finding decides an internal dispute in teleosemantics: etiological versus stabilization-based content.

## S5. Exact evidence and computable rationality (from C08)

**Thesis.** In models where agents learn exact real-valued quantities, as in Kolmogorov conditionalization (Rescorla) and much Bayesian cognitive science, no computable update rule satisfies the conditionalization norm even almost everywhere (Ackerman, Freer and Roy). So either computable agents are irrational by those models' own lights, or noisy, finite-precision updating is normatively basic rather than an approximation. The paper argues for the second horn. Jeffrey-style or noise-channel updating is the correct norm, and exact conditionalization is its idealized limit, not the other way round.

**Argument.**
(1) The relevant models state the norm as an almost-everywhere constraint on update rules for continuous evidence.
(2) There are computable priors for which no computable rule satisfies it.
(3) A norm unsatisfiable by every computable agent cannot be the basic norm for such agents, and noisy updating is computable under mild conditions (Ackerman, Freer and Roy's positive result).

**Concessions.** For discrete or finite-precision evidence, conditionalization is computable. The thesis concerns the exact-evidence idealization only. It must engage Lopez-Wild (2026) on computable Bayesian epistemology.

**Why surprising.** It reverses the usual order of idealization in Bayesian epistemology.

## S6. What liars can do that misleaders cannot (from C05)

**Thesis.** Take hearers who believe what is asserted but discount what is merely implied. Against them, a liar can induce posterior distributions that are not Bayes-plausible: systematic false belief in every state. A committed misleader, using only true assertions and implicatures, is confined to a strictly smaller set. Characterizing this gap gives an epistemic basis for the moral asymmetry between lying and misleading: lying can produce miscalibration, and misleading cannot, against such hearers.

**Argument.**
(1) Hearers trust assertions and treat implicatures as defeasible inferences that they partly discount.
(2) Under (1), the beliefs reachable by true assertions with strategic implicature are bounded by a calibration constraint, while lies are not so bounded.
(3) The difference is exactly the region of systematically false, miscalibrated belief, a harm that only lying can impose.

**Concessions.** The resulting epistemic states differ, so this reverses C05. It must go beyond Thorstad's "Exploitative informing" and Sobel (2020).

**Why surprising.** It gives a formal, harm-based defence of the asymmetry that Saul attacked.

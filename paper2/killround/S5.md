REFUTATION REPORT: S5, "Exact evidence and computable rationality"

Search limits: I could not fetch full texts. philsci-archive, arxiv, ar5iv and danroy.org all fail DNS in this sandbox, so everything below comes from search-result snippets plus what I already know of the Ackerman, Freer and Roy (AFR) paper. I used 12 searches.

(a) Prior work
- **No exact pre-emption found.** No published or preprinted paper turned up that argues noisy or Jeffrey updating is normatively basic because exact conditionalization is noncomputable. No source in the searches connects Rescorla's Kolmogorov conditionalization to AFR, and I found no Mercier, Lopez-Wild and Spiegel 2026 paper.
- **The argument pattern is already published.** Lopez-Wild, "Computable Bayesian Epistemology" (Phil Sci 2026, doi 10.1017/psa.2026.10273; PhilSci preprint 23862, 2024), argues that computable agents cannot have merely finitely additive credences, so countable additivity is the right norm. It positions itself against Carr's defence of ideal epistemology and engages Thorstad. That is the same move: use computability to choose between rival norms, and answer the ideal-agent objection. Applying it to updating is a natural next step but not a new kind of argument. I could not see whether he discusses AFR.
- **Nearby work goes a different way.** Huttegger, Walsh and Zaffora Blando ("Bayesian Convergence for Computably Bounded Agents", PhilSci 27049, 2025; arXiv 2403.19978 on "effective disintegrations") model computably bounded Bayesians by assuming computable priors with computable disintegrations. They do not revise the updating norm. This is the third option S5's dilemma leaves out (see b1).
- **Jeffrey on continuous partitions needs regular conditional distributions.** Meehan and Zhang, "Jeffrey Meets Kolmogorov" (J Phil Logic), show that Jeffrey conditionalization generalised to continuous partitions requires regular conditional distributions. It has Kolmogorov conditionalization as a special case. So the "Jeffrey horn" inherits the AFR noncomputability; it does not escape it.

(b) Premises and inference
1. **Quantifier shift (fatal to step 3).**
   - Premise (2) is true: there are computable priors with no computable version of the posterior kernel.
   - Premise (3) needs something stronger: that the norm is unsatisfiable by every computable agent. That does not follow.
   - For the large class of computable priors whose conditionals are computable, a computable agent satisfies Kolmogorov conditionalization exactly. AFR's own positive results (discrete or dominated cases, bounded computable conditional densities, de Finetti/exchangeable cases) show this.
   - So the noncomputability is a property of particular priors, not of the norm. The obvious conclusion is "computable rationality constrains priors". That is exactly Lopez-Wild's and Huttegger-Walsh-Zaffora Blando's strategy, and it leaves conditionalization untouched.
   - The dilemma "computable agents are irrational, or noisy updating is basic" is therefore false.
2. **Noisy updating is exact conditionalization (fatal to the "reversal").**
   - AFR's positive result conditions exactly on the real-valued observation Y = X + ε, where ε is independent computable noise with a bounded, suitably smooth computable density.
   - That is Kolmogorov conditionalization on a continuous quantity, under a prior (the joint over X and ε) that meets a regularity condition. It is not finite-precision updating, and it is not a rival norm.
   - So the "noisy horn" is not an alternative to exact conditionalization. It is exact conditionalization with a restricted prior, which is objection 1 again.
   - "Finite-precision" updating, meaning conditioning on rational intervals of positive probability, is plain ratio conditionalization. The proposal itself concedes that this case is computable.
3. **(ii) Is premise (2) exactly what AFR prove? Broadly yes, and more.** They show every version of the conditional distribution is noncomputable, so the "a.e." wording is not an overstatement. Search snippets say the result extends to the weaker L¹(P_X) sense of computability. The example is "almost continuous but not almost computable", and its conditional encodes the halting problem. The result is for a fixed prior, which is stronger than a claim about a uniform update operator. The premise is accurate. The trouble is what the proposal draws from it.
4. **(i) The ought-implies-can reply is not answered.** Standard Bayesian ideals (logical omniscience, coherence over infinite algebras) already demand far more than computable agents can do. The defenders of ideal epistemology that Lopez-Wild engages (Carr, Smithies) deny that ideal norms must be satisfiable. The proposal states premise (3) without arguing for it. If premise (3) were sound, it would overturn much more of Bayesianism than conditionalization, and the proposal says nothing about where it stops.
5. **(iii) The noise conditions are idealized too.**
   - The agent must have an exactly computable noise density with known bounds, independent of the signal.
   - It must still receive Y as an exact real, through an oracle supplying infinitely many approximations.
   - These assumptions are about as idealized as the ones being rejected. They just come out computable.
6. **(iv) "Reverses the order of idealization" is either wrong or already standard.**
   - In AFR's counterexample the exact conditional is everywhere discontinuous. So noisy posteriors converge to it, as noise goes to zero, only in a weak almost-everywhere sense, and not computably. Calling exact conditionalization a well-behaved "limit" of noisy updating is therefore shaky.
   - Where the limit does behave well, "exact conditionalization is an idealization of noisy or finite measurement" is not a reversal. It is the ordinary view in statistics and measurement theory, and it is the core of Jeffrey's radical probabilism (certainty-learning is an idealization).

(c) Triviality
Once objections 1 and 2 are granted, the thesis reduces to two familiar claims:
- Computability constrains which priors an agent can have.
- Real measurements are noisy.

Neither is new.

VERDICT: KILLED

(1) Decisive reasons
- The central inference is a quantifier shift. Some computable priors have noncomputable posteriors, but that does not make the norm unsatisfiable for all computable agents. The natural fix is a constraint on priors, already the strategy of Lopez-Wild and of Huttegger-Walsh-Zaffora Blando, and it leaves conditionalization untouched.
- The proposed rival norm, AFR's noisy updating, is itself exact Kolmogorov conditionalization on a real-valued variable under a smoothness condition on the prior. So there is no reversal of norms.
- The Jeffrey alternative, generalised to continuous partitions, needs regular conditional distributions and inherits the same noncomputability.
- The ought-implies-can premise is asserted against an established ideal-epistemology literature, not defended.
- What remains ("exact evidence is an idealization") is the standard view.

(2) What a successor would have to become
A paper on which computability constraints on priors are rationally required. Conjecture: computable rationality requires priors whose disintegrations are computable, for example via a built-in smoothing noise channel. The paper would then ask whether that requirement is principled or ad hoc, compared with Lopez-Wild's computability-based case for countable additivity. It would have to:
- engage Lopez-Wild 2026, Huttegger-Walsh-Zaffora Blando 2025 and Meehan-Zhang;
- defend a restricted ought-implies-can principle for norms that bear on the agent's own updating;
- stop calling noisy updating a distinct norm.

Even then it would be a modest extension of existing work, not a surprising reversal.

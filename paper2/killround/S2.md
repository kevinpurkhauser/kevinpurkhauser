Access caveat: I could not fetch any full text. philsci-archive.pitt.edu, arxiv.org and preprints.org do not resolve from this session, so they would need to be allowed in the session's network settings. Everything below comes from search-result abstracts and snippets. The claims about Murialdo et al. rest on their abstract only.

(a) Prior work / foil check

1. The proposal gets its foil wrong. This is the most serious problem. Murialdo, Céspedes and Cifuentes, "A Deductive Argument for Strong Emergence Based on The Halting Problem" (PhilSci-Archive 25933, July 2025), is not mainly an argument from the Gu et al. or Cubitt–Perez-Garcia–Wolf undecidability results. Their abstract says that a recent result in theoretical computer science lets answers to the Halting Problem be verified even when there is no plausible way to derive them bottom-up. That is almost certainly MIP* = RE (Ji, Natarajan, Vidick, Wright, Yuen). They set out six sufficient criteria for strong emergence within a digital-physics framework, claim five are actual, and support the sixth by analogy and a Boltzmann-brain case study. So:
   - Their key notion is verification without bottom-up derivation. It is not Gödel-independence of a physical gap property.
   - The proposal's "theory-relativity" and "quantifier-order" points target independence of a sentence from a theory, which this argument may not use at all. There is also a structural mismatch. MIP* = RE certifies halting instances, which are Σ1 and so always provable in PA by running the machine. Any "no bottom-up route" claim must therefore be about feasibility or physical access, not in-principle deducibility. Refuting that needs a different diagnosis from the three offered.
   - Unless the full text leans heavily on the spectral-gap results (not verified), the paper as pitched refutes an argument nobody made in that form.
   - I found no published replies to Murialdo et al.

2. The core negative thesis is already published. Humphreys (2015), "More is Different… Sometimes: Ising Models, Emergence, and Undecidability" (in *Why More Is Different*, Springer, pp. 137–152), concludes that the Barahona and Gu et al. undecidability results "do not provide evidence for the existence of ontologically emergent states." On his reading they bear on weak or inferential emergence and on the limits of certain theoretical representations. Ippoliti and Caprara (2021), "Undecidability of the Spectral Gap: An Epistemological Look" (*J. Gen. Phil. Sci.*), already reads the Cubitt results epistemically and questions how representational the models are. Bedau's "Weak Emergence" had already used Game-of-Life undecidability to support weak over strong emergence. The conclusion "undecidability gives computational or proof-theoretic limits, not strong emergence" is standard.

3. The quantifier-order point is in Cubitt et al.'s own statement of the result. Their phrasing is: "given any consistent recursive axiomatization of mathematics, there exist particular quantum many-body Hamiltonians for which the presence or absence of the spectral gap is not determined by these axioms" (∀T ∃H). The authors also stress that the undecidability concerns the general problem, not individual instances. Aaronson noted, as quoted in a public discussion thread, that Seth Lloyd (1993) already had gapped-if-halts constructions. So the "for each theory some H, but no H independent of all theories" observation is the theorem's stated form plus textbook Gödel logic. Theory-relativity (independence is removable in a stronger sound theory) is also standard. Neither can carry the claimed novelty over Humphreys.

(b) Premise and inference problems

(i) What criterion Murialdo et al. use: I could not confirm it. Their six sufficient criteria are framed around verifiability without bottom-up derivability, not around formal independence. The proposal's account ("the recent argument that [Gu/Cubitt] ground strong emergence") is unsupported and probably wrong.

(ii) The level-neutrality test fails in two ways.
   - Counterexample: in Gu et al. and Cubitt et al., the gap is decidable for every finite lattice, since those are finite matrices. Non-derivability appears only in the thermodynamic limit. The unbounded quantifier over system size L in the arithmetical encoding is exactly the micro-to-macro passage. So the non-derivability does attach to the passage between levels, at least on limit-based views of emergence (Batterman; Gu et al. explicitly). Premise (2) is false, or at best begs the question against them.
   - It proves too much: any failure of deducibility that is fixed by the base plus mathematics can be encoded arithmetically. The test therefore rules out by stipulation every formalizable emergence claim, which begs the question against emergentists who hold that levels matter through exactly such encodings.

(iii) Quantifier order: yes, it is already in Cubitt et al. (see a3). Humphreys' abstract does not show it, but his conclusion does not need it.

(iv) Premise (1) misrepresents the literature.
   - Wilson's strong emergence is a novel-powers or causal criterion, not a deducibility criterion, so undecidability is irrelevant to it from the start.
   - Chalmers' strong emergence is failure of in-principle deducibility, meaning a priori entailment by an ideal reasoner. That is not provability in a fixed recursive theory, and mathematical truths count as a priori. Gödel-independence from a given T therefore never threatens Chalmers-deducibility. This makes the negative thesis close to trivial for Chalmers; he himself classes Game-of-Life-style unpredictability as weak emergence.
   - The clause "because of the base-to-higher-level relation" is the proposal's own addition, not Chalmers' or Wilson's. Once it is added, (3) follows almost by definition.

(c) Triviality: on the two standard accounts the result is either trivial (Chalmers: a priori entailment is untouched by relative independence) or irrelevant (Wilson: powers, not deducibility). The conclusion matches Humphreys (2015). The diagnostic tools are standard logic, and one of them (quantifier order) is in the source theorem.

VERDICT: KILLED (as framed)

(1) Decisive reasons:
   - The named foil (Murialdo et al. 2025) appears to argue from MIP* = RE-style verification of halting, not from spectral-gap independence, so the three diagnostics probably miss it.
   - The thesis that undecidability doesn't ground ontological or strong emergence is Humphreys' published conclusion, with Ippoliti and Caprara and Bedau in support.
   - The quantifier-order point is how Cubitt et al. themselves state the theorem.
   - The level-neutrality test fails on the thermodynamic limit and begs the question against limit-based emergentists.
   - Premise (1) misreads Wilson and makes the Chalmers case trivial.

(2) What it would have to become to survive: a direct, focused reply to Murialdo et al. on their actual argument. That means checking the full text, then:
   - showing that MIP* = RE verification concerns Σ1 facts, which are always deducible in principle, so "no bottom-up route" is only a feasibility claim and gives weak or Bedau emergence at most;
   - evaluating their six criteria against the Chalmers a priori entailment standard, and saying explicitly why Wilson-style accounts are unaffected.
   Separately, a more ambitious paper could take the thermodynamic-limit objection head on, asking whether the unbounded size quantifier is a genuine inter-level relation. That would engage Batterman, Butterfield and Gu et al. rather than assuming level-neutrality.

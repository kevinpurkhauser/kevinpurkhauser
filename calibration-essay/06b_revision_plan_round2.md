# 06b. Revision plan after referee round 2

Round 2 (fresh referees): A 7/5/8, B 7/5/7, C 7/6/7; all major revision. All concede the central negative point (the luck/fragility assumption behind the modal and evidence-conditioning replies is false). New substantive objections (not raised in round 1): A3 false under the exogenous reading; quantifier gap for coin-fixed processes over rich ranges; R's same-input clause vacuous (B); normal-worlds misformulation (B); demon comparison wrong (B); Hu-Shmaya computability exception (C); order-of-explanation reply (A, B); exhaustiveness gaps (A, B).

Responses adopted:
1. History h = outcomes, labels, index. Propensity is the unconditional average over coins. Fix the 2/7 derivation.
2. Main argument by broad individuation: calibration in a world is almost-sure calibration under that world's chances and the process's coins (the only reading on which any process, including chance-matchers, can be calibrated throughout a rich range). Reliability of a randomized rule is assessed by chance-expected excess Brier inaccuracy. Lemma (a) then suffices; no limsup issue; holds at every finite horizon. Narrow individuation treated in one paragraph (actual-run standards only; Lemma (b)). Flagship: deterministic Foster-Hart case, where no individuation issue arises.
3. R reformulated: chance given inputs must be computable from those inputs relative to the process's own rule (same inputs, same resources). Non-vacuous; excludes the hidden-sequence world; makes 5.1 and 5.2 consistent. A4 kept only to show the propensity-binning calibrationist must enrich the family, leading to 5.2. Requires finitely many rational coins per day for the witnesses; stated.
4. A3 replaced by the corrected bound P(propensity-binned calibration in w_rho) <= 5/9 (C's argument, checked); tolerance stated.
5. Normal worlds: restate the target honestly. The first horn refutes world-local calibrationism (actual, nearby; any range containing @). For normal-worlds calibrationism, it shows only that calibration across normal worlds does not secure reliability in each normal world; aggregate normal-world reliability is left as a limit.
6. Justification vs reliability: conclusion stated primarily about calibration as an account of reliability; the justification conclusion is conditional on reliabilism's own link; supply the barometer case and a natural persistence-world case as intuitive support.
7. Repertoire relativization (B): added to 6.1.
8. Demon: corrected. R says demon victims' processes are unreliable in the demon world, as standard reliabilism does; calibrationism's distinctive verdict is reliability even there, which overgenerates (barometer).
9. Order of explanation: argument added (derivation direction; borrowed discriminating power; reductive Dawidian concession: then the view is the positive view under a reductive theory of chance, not D2). Finite-stretch asymmetry removed.
10. Exhaustiveness: "dilemma for the main forms", plus treatment of calibration plus refinement, zero tolerance with refining grids (horn 1), deterministic sharp over rich ranges, unconditional cross-world calibration, rate-sensitive standards (falls under (G)(i) via Sandroni's finite-horizon theorem but only with infeasible manipulators; open under computational bounds).
11. Hu-Shmaya 2013 added to section 7 and 5.2; (G)'s scope stated (non-computable manipulators allowed).
12. Natural case first: persistence world.
13. Literature: Putnam 1963 (diagonal ancestor), Seidenfeld 1985, Gneiting et al. 2007, Williams-Pettigrew, Clifton (statistical reliabilism) for section 8, Zhao 2022 not needed. Belot 2013 cited.
14. Drop unproved "near one half" claim. Define "uninformed". Dawid 1982 pages 605-610.

# Round 1, Referee B (reliabilist sympathetic to Dunn)

Record of the report (condensed by the author agent; every substantive point retained).

Verdict: MAJOR REVISION. Scores: originality 8, correctness 5, importance 7.

Concedes: the paper establishes that calibration of realized outputs, on reference classes fixed independently of the process, assessed world-locally, is not sufficient for reliability, and that the reason is a theorem. Rejects the generality claims.

## Replies and assessment
A. (Most serious; not defeated.) Process-relative reference classes / calibration of expected credence: D- = {f-bar < 1/2}, D+ = complement, or {f-bar in I}. In w_rho, rho fails calibration on these; chance-matcher passes any countable predictable family. So not securable. Natural, not ad hoc: a process type is a map from inputs to output distributions; the paper itself uses expected distance. For deterministic processes these are coarse bins of realized output; the Foster-Hart smooth witness fails sharp coarse calibration in its own diagonal world. Escapes section 6 (dispositional, not "credences actually formed"). Needs: argue illegitimate, or show insufficient for another reason, or restrict thesis.
B. (Essential.) Computability: computable rho with rational coin biases gives computable f-bar, computable w_rho, computable checking rule D-. No computable process calibrated on all computable rules in every world. Dawid 1985: forecasters calibrated on all computable selection rules agree asymptotically; pushes toward chance-matching in computable worlds. Section 6 manipulators may be non-computable. Goldman's reliabilism concerns psychological processes, plausibly computable.
C. (Not defeated.) Equivocation: reliability IN w (R) vs reliability AT w (CR-S consequent). Normal-worlds calibrationism accepts R and says the witness is justified in w_rho because reliable in normal worlds; in normal worlds whose chance structure is measurable w.r.t. R, calibration on R forces asymptotic chance-matching. Restrict target to world-local CR-S or argue separately against normal-worlds calibrationism (barometer case best material).
D. (Partly defeated.) R is not weak: chance given full history is close to the indicator view; near question-begging. Better: chance-free version from realized long-run Brier: chance-matcher 0.09 a.s., rho at least about 0.25 a.s. (martingale LLN); a track-record fact. This strengthens 5.1 but undercuts "not a track-record property at all".
E. (Not defeated for conditional calibrationism.) Demon: conditional calibrationism already handles the barometer; the counterexample arises only where chance structure is not measurable w.r.t. R; relative to the agent's categories w_rho is a demon world. Rare-cell case is the familiar single-case problem; theorem plays no role there.
F. (Defeatable, not defeated.) "Only a process encoding rho does well in w_rho": if rho computable, w_rho is computable; a Bayesian with prior weight on it merges (Blackwell-Dubins), Solomonoff-type predictor converges. So reliability attainable in w_rho by general learners. Add this.
G. (Essential for 6.) Individuation: Sandroni/Shmaya witnesses draw a theory then forecast deterministically; narrowly individuated ("follow theta"), the process has its own diagonal world and the lemma's bound (expectation over theta) does not apply. Principled (agent who acquired prior by lottery is assessed as a Bayesian with that prior). "Any natural type inherits calibration" is backwards (subtypes fixing coins do not; broader types contain miscalibrated members). Daily randomization: narrow individuation by coin sequence less natural, not obviously unprincipled.
H. Randomized credence: against sharp calibration only randomized witnesses exist; assessing by expected credence (Reply A) blocks them. FV randomizes between adjacent grid points, so "true" credence effectively f-bar. Scorecard-balancing restriction partly defeated (recalibration from one's record is common practice; section 6 witnesses do not use own past forecasts).
I. (Partly.) Graded/finite: at realistic horizons uniform guarantees are weak (T^{-1/3} vs T^{-1/2} for chance-matcher); graded calibrationist ranks them correctly at finite T. Rates l1 vs l2; T^{-1/3} vs Dagan et al.
J. Trichotomy: all measurable classes unsatisfiable (hindsight rules); classes fixed independently of process: securable, argument works; process-relative: not securable, argument fails. Make explicit.
K. (Not defeated.) Comparative: false dilemma; middle option: rich class C (all computable processes) compared on realized records; computable rho fails in w_rho. Mischaracterization: Al-Najjar-Weinstein's test is record-based; it is the success guarantee that presupposes an informed expert. Discuss Feinberg-Stewart.
L. Definitional reply: defeated, especially with chance-free realized-Brier point.

## Replies
1. (Most important, not answered.) R's second conjunct is vacuous: the map h -> c(h) always exists, so R is unrestricted chance-closeness; A4 competitor encodes an arbitrary sequence yet R counts it, while 5.2 calls the same verdict an application of D1: inconsistent. Repair: admissibility / learnability clause (e.g. a computable learner whose r.e. hypothesis class includes w converges). Then the first horn still holds for computable witnesses, but the A4 route against propensity-binning is lost.
2. (Not addressed.) Relativize reliability to the agent's repertoire R: in w_{sigma_R} chances depend on a feature invisible to R; D1 read as predictability by rules in R. Defeat requires: generalized 6.1 point (for each R some process maximally reliable everywhere), realized Brier margin, and a fourth branch (R chosen world-relatively imports chance).
3. (Not defeated.) Normal-worlds reliabilism grounds justification in reliability across normal worlds, not in @; step 6 attacks a straw position; FV converges in i.i.d. normal worlds; need a substantial class of normal worlds where the witness is unreliable, or a direct argument about justification. "Can be normal" asserted; Goldman's normal worlds fixed by our general beliefs; a threshold-on-propensity rain law looks like a "coincidence demon" world.
4. (Not defeated.) Demon comparison false: in a demon world the truth is predictable from inputs (chance of hand given inputs about 0), so R condemns the victim; the paper's view needs normal-worlds relativization too. FV does respond to its evidence.
5. (Partly.) Conspiratorial worlds; lead with a natural case: Markov persistence world (chance 0.9 if rained yesterday, else 0.1; stationary 1/2); a forecaster calibrated only on its own bins can sit near 1/2; "same as yesterday" tracks.
6. Individuation: coin-fixed witness encodes an arbitrary sequence, which 5.1 lets the calibrationist reject; consistency needed. Table of individuations (deterministic FH; randomized assessed by realized credences; randomized assessed by propensity). "Deliberately hedging to keep my record calibrated" is a psychological type a calibrationist may call reliable.
7. (Real gap.) No intuitive verdict that the witness's credences are unjustified; R does all the work; close to question-begging against the calibrationist about justification. Provide a case (agent ignoring an evident pattern, hedging to keep record) or restrict conclusion to accounts of reliability.
8. (Not defeated.) Order of explanation: Dawid-style empirical probability, hypothetical or modal frequentism define probability from calibration; horn 2 is then a reductive success; co-extension is not grounding. Asymmetry: 5.2 objects that computable calibration constrains only the limit, while section 8 accepts unverifiability for its own view.
9. 10. 5.1 verdict unfair (hidden ML-random chances: one-half process as reliable as any learner can be).

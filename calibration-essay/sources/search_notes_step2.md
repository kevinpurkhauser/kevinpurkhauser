# Search notes for Step 2 (verbatim snippets from abstracts and secondary sources)

- Foster and Vohra 1998, Biometrika 85: 379-390. Foster discussion paper: "Foster and Vohra exhibit a randomized forecasting algorithm that almost surely will be calibrated on all sequences." Theorem 1 snippet: "A randomized forecast using the mu_t defined by equation (2) with k approx 1/eps^2 is an eps-calibrated forecast" (OCR uncertain). Survey: forecasts take one of m fixed values; approximately calibrated for m large. Secondary: "asymptotic calibration can be achieved without any knowledge of what the state will be in each round"; "If the algorithm predicts deterministically, the adversary can pick a state that forces a calibration error of at least 1/2." Adversary model: "Nature is often modeled as an adaptive adversary who sees all activity of the forecaster except the randomization that the forecaster may deploy."
- Foster 1999, "A proof of calibration via Blackwell's approachability theorem": abstract lists Foster-Vohra 1998, Hart 1995 (personal communication), Fudenberg and Levine 1999, Hart and Mas-Colell as other proofs.
- Qiao and Valiant, STOC 2021: lower bound Omega(T^0.528) on calibration error; O(T^{2/3}) long-standing upper bound. Dagan et al. (arXiv 2406.13668; STOC 2025, pp. 2007-2018): first improvement on O(T^{2/3}); reports lower bound Omega(T^0.54389) against oblivious adversary.
- Kakade and Foster, COLT 2004 / JCSS 2008, "Deterministic calibration and Nash equilibrium": deterministic weakly calibrated algorithm. Foster and Hart 2017 preprint: weak calibration introduced by Kakade and Foster 2004 and Foster and Kakade 2006; weak and smooth calibration essentially equivalent.
- Foster and Hart, GEB 109 (2018): 271-293: "While regular calibration can be guaranteed only by randomized forecasting procedures, we show that smooth calibration can be guaranteed by deterministic procedures." Leaked forecasts: "smooth calibration can nevertheless be guaranteed (while regular calibration cannot)." "our procedure has finite recall, is stationary, and all forecasts lie on a finite grid."
- Sandroni, Smorodinsky, Vohra 2003, MOR 28(1): 141-153: "given any countable partition of [0,1] and any countable collection of subsequences, ... construct a forecasting scheme whose long-run average forecast matches the long-run frequency of realized outcomes on every subsequence, for all infinite data strings" (search paraphrase of abstract). doi 10.1287/moor.28.1.141.14264.
- Foster, Rakhlin, Sridharan, Tewari, COLT 2011, PMLR 19: 293-314: "complexity of the family of checking rules can be captured by the notion of a sequential cover ... upper bounds on the value of the associated game are proved, thus certifying the existence of a calibrated strategy"; assumptions include finiteness of VC and Littlestone dimensions.
- Hebert-Johnson, Kim, Reingold, Rothblum, ICML 2018 (PMLR 80: 1939-1948): "guarantees meaningful (calibrated) predictions for every subpopulation that can be identified within a specified class of computations."
- Gupta, Jung, Noarov, Pai, Roth, "Online multivalid learning: means, moments, and prediction intervals" (arXiv 2101.01739): online multicalibration (title and topic only).
- Sandroni 2003, IJGT 32: 151-159, doi 10.1007/s001820300153: "Based on Fan's (1953) Minimax theorem, I show that it is possible to produce forecasts that will not be rejected on any sequence of outcomes." Pomatto 2021 (TE 16: 129-160): "any test that operates in finite time and is free of Type I errors can be passed by a strategic but uninformed forecaster."
- Olszewski and Sandroni 2009, MOR 34: 57-70, doi 10.1287/moor.1080.0347: with no restriction on the domain of permissible theories, strategic experts cannot be discredited for an arbitrary but given number of periods, no matter which test is used (provided the test does not reject the actual data-generating process); ways around: unbounded data sets, restricting the domain; "only to a limited extent".
- Olszewski and Sandroni 2008, Econometrica 76: 1437-1466: "A test can be ignorantly passed if a false expert, with no knowledge of the data-generating process, can pass the test"; "tests which cannot be ignorantly passed must rely on predictions about data not yet observed, and such tests cannot be run if forecasters report only the probability of the next period's events on the basis of the actually observed data." Future-independence (Shmaya's description): the decision whether to reject at some period does not depend on predictions made for later periods.
- Shmaya 2008, TE 3: 367-382: "If the test function is Borel and the inspection is such that a true expert always passes it, then it is also manipulable by an ignorant expert." "The proof uses Martin's theorem about the determinacy of Blackwell games." "Under the axiom of choice, there exist non-Borel test functions that are not manipulable."
- Hu and Shmaya 2013, TE 8: a test not manipulable by any computable strategies that accepts true experts with high probabilities.
- Olszewski and Sandroni 2009, Annals of Statistics 37(2): 1013-1039, doi 10.1214/08-AOS597: "Many tests proposed in the existing literature, including calibration tests, control for type I error but are manipulable. We construct a test that controls for type I error and is nonmanipulable." Global category test; does not need the continuum hypothesis (unlike Dekel-Feinberg).
- Dekel and Feinberg 2006, RES 73(4): 893-906: test of a prediction of the distribution of a stochastic process; small set notion = category I.
- Feinberg and Stewart 2008, Econometrica 76: 561-582: false experts fail on all but a small (category I) set of true distributions.
- Fortnow and Vohra 2009, Econometrica 77(1): 93-105: "a linear-time test and distribution of nature such that any forecaster without knowledge of the future who can fool the test must be able to solve computationally difficult problems"; tech report: PSPACE-hard.
- Al-Najjar and Weinstein 2008, Econometrica 76(3): 541-559: test "can always determine which of two experts knows the true distribution", "achieves any desired degree of precision in some fixed finite time, and does not use counterfactual predictions"; "this paradox arises because in the single-expert setting, any mixed strategy for Nature over distributions is reducible to a pure strategy"; "the presence of an expert who knows the realized distribution eliminates the reducibility of Nature's compound lotteries."
- Al-Najjar, Sandroni, Smorodinsky, Weinstein 2010, JET 145(6): 2203-2217: expert names a date by which she will have learned enough for a sharp forecast; accepts informed expert, not manipulable by uninformed; no such test for unrestricted class.
- Oakes 1985, JASA 80: 339-342: self-calibrating priors do not exist; quotes Dawid: a forecaster cannot adjust future predictions to correct past miscalibration without sacrificing coherence.
- Dawid 1985, Annals of Statistics 13(4): 1251-1274: any two sequences of forecasts meeting the (computable-subsequence) calibration criterion must be in asymptotic agreement.
